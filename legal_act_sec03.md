The Grand Jury, having reviewed documentary evidence, financial records, official filings, court records, and investigative archives, hereby returns findings of fact and legal qualification concerning the systematic concealment of global wealth through offshore financial infrastructure. ## COUNT I: THE PANDORA PAPERS — DOCUMENTED CONCEALMENT BY WORLD LEADERS AND BILLIONAIRES

### FINDING OF FACT 1.1

On 3 October 2021, the International Consortium of Investigative Journalists (ICIJ) published the Pandora Papers, comprising 11.9 million documents totaling 2.94 terabytes of data sourced from 14 offshore service providers across secrecy jurisdictions. The dataset exposed the offshore holdings of 35 current and former world leaders, more than 330 politicians and public officials, and over 130 billionaires from 45 countries, including 46 Russian oligarchs.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 1.1

The ICIJ maintains the Offshore Leaks database in active integration with OpenSanctions, as confirmed by records processed through 2026. The 11.9 million documents were reviewed by more than 600 journalists across 117 countries. The 14 offshore service providers named in the leak include Alcogal (Panama), which alone created at least 14,000 shell companies and trusts in tax havens.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 1.1

The creation and maintenance of shell companies and trusts in secrecy jurisdictions for the purpose of concealing beneficial ownership constitutes tax evasion, money laundering, and fraud under the laws of every jurisdiction where the underlying income was earned. The 130+ billionaires and 35 world leaders identified in the Pandora Papers are subjects of documented criminal conduct. The failure to prosecute in all cases establishes prosecutorial failure as a separate institutional offense.

### DEMAND/ORDER 1.1

All 14 offshore service providers identified in the Pandora Papers shall produce complete client lists, beneficial ownership registries, and transaction records for the period 2010–2026. All 35 world leaders and 130+ billionaires named in the leak shall file amended tax returns disclosing all previously concealed assets, or face indictment for tax evasion and money laundering.

## COUNT II: NAMED INDIVIDUALS — DOCUMENTED OFFSHORE CONCEALMENT

### FINDING OF FACT 2.1

King Abdullah II of Jordan was identified as the beneficiary of 16 companies established by the Panamanian law firm Alcogal, which held a $100 million property empire spanning Malibu, Washington D.C., London's Belgravia, and Ascot. A 2017 Alcogal memorandum named a client "Abdullah Al Hussein," born 30 January 1962 — the King's birth date — residing at Raghadan Palace, as beneficiary of the 16 corporate vehicles. The Jordanian royal court denied misuse of public funds; ICIJ access was blocked in Jordan hours before publication.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 2.1

The Alcogal memorandum is documentary evidence of beneficial ownership. The 16 corporate vehicles are registered in secrecy jurisdictions. The $100 million property empire is documented by land registry records in four jurisdictions.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 2.1

The use of 16 shell companies to hold $100 million in real estate by a sitting head of state constitutes prima facie evidence of money laundering and undisclosed enrichment. The blocking of ICIJ access constitutes obstruction of justice. Immunity for heads of state does not extend to criminal financial conduct under customary international law.

### DEMAND/ORDER 2.1

King Abdullah II shall produce documentary evidence of the lawful source of funds used to acquire the $100 million property empire, including tax returns and banking documentation for the period 2010–2017. Failure to produce such evidence shall result in the issuance of an international arrest warrant for money laundering and the freezing of all identified assets.

---

### FINDING OF FACT 2.2

Former United Kingdom Prime Minister Tony Blair appeared in the Pandora Papers through a British Virgin Islands company, Romanstone International Limited, which owned a London building purchased for £6.45 million. The arrangement allowed Blair to avoid £312,000 in stamp duty. Alcogal denied that either Tony or Cherie Blair were its clients, claiming ownership had transferred before their involvement.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 2.2

The BVI company registration for Romanstone International Limited is documentary evidence. The £6.45 million purchase price and £312,000 stamp duty avoidance are calculable from UK property transaction records. The Alcogal denial is contradicted by the leaked documents naming the Blairs as beneficial owners.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 2.2

The use of a BVI shell company to acquire UK real estate for the purpose of avoiding stamp duty constitutes tax fraud under the UK Finance Act and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002. The £312,000 in avoided duty is a quantified loss to the UK Treasury.

### DEMAND/ORDER 2.2

Tony Blair and Cherie Blair shall pay the £312,000 in avoided stamp duty, plus penalties and interest calculated at HMRC statutory rates. Romanstone International Limited shall be dissolved and its assets repatriated to UK jurisdiction. The UK Crown Prosecution Service shall review the file for prosecution under the Fraud Act 2006.

---

### FINDING OF FACT 2.3

Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš was documented using €15 million transferred between his offshore companies to acquire a chateau on the French Riviera. He failed to declare the offshore vehicle as required by Czech law and dismissed the leak as a smear attempt.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 2.3

The €15 million transfer is documented in Alcogal files. The French Riviera chateau acquisition is recorded in French land registry. The failure to declare the offshore vehicle is established by Czech asset disclosure requirements for public officials.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 2.3

The failure to declare offshore assets by a sitting Prime Minister constitutes criminal breach of trust and tax evasion. The use of €15 million in undeclared offshore funds to acquire personal real estate constitutes embezzlement of public or corporate funds, pending source-of-funds verification.

### DEMAND/ORDER 2.3

Andrej Babiš shall produce complete banking records for the €15 million transfer, including source-of-funds documentation. The Czech Prosecutor General shall initiate criminal proceedings for tax evasion and breach of trust. The French authorities shall place a judicial lien on the Riviera chateau pending resolution.

## COUNT III: CYPRUS CONFIDENTIAL — SANCTIONS EVASION AND WARTIME ASSET TRANSFERS

### FINDING OF FACT 3.1

In November 2023, the ICIJ and Paper Trail Media published the Cyprus Confidential investigation, analyzing 3.6 million leaked documents from six Cyprus-based financial services providers and a Latvian firm. Among the 104 Russian billionaires on the 2023 Forbes list, 67 — two-thirds — appeared as clients of Cypriot professional services providers, along with their family members.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 3.1

The 3.6 million documents were reviewed by 270 journalists from 54 countries. The 67 Russian billionaires are identified by name in the ICIJ database. The timing establishes that Cypriot service providers continued to service Russian oligarch clients after the invasion and the imposition of initial sanctions.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 3.1

The provision of offshore services to sanctioned Russian oligarchs after February 2022 constitutes sanctions evasion under EU Regulation 833/2014, UK Russia (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019, and U.S. Executive Order 14066. The Cypriot professional service providers who facilitated these structures are co-conspirators in sanctions evasion.

### DEMAND/ORDER 3.1

The Republic of Cyprus shall revoke the operating licenses of all six financial services providers named in the Cyprus Confidential leak and shall prosecute their directors and compliance officers for sanctions evasion. The EU shall impose secondary sanctions on all 67 Russian billionaires identified in the leak who maintained Cyprus structures after February 2022.

---

### FINDING OF FACT 3.2

PwC Cyprus helped sanctioned oligarchs shuffle their riches as Russian tanks rolled into Ukraine. In spring 2022, scientists-turned-billionaires Alexander Abramov and Alexander Frolov rushed to move $100 million between two shell companies they controlled, with the original source traced to Evraz PLC, the steel and mining giant producing 97 percent of the metal used in Russian railways. Two months after the transaction, the British government sanctioned Evraz as "of strategic significance to the government of Russia."

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 3.2

The $100 million transfer is documented in Cyprus Confidential files. The Evraz PLC connection is established by corporate registry records. The British government sanctions designation is published in the UK Sanctions List. The two-month gap establishes that the transfer was executed while the oligarchs had knowledge of impending sanctions.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 3.2

The $100 million transfer constitutes sanctions evasion and money laundering. PwC Cyprus's facilitation of the transfer while Russian military forces were engaged in the invasion of Ukraine constitutes complicity in sanctions evasion and potentially material support for a hostile military power.

### DEMAND/ORDER 3.2

PwC Cyprus shall be criminally indicted for sanctions evasion. All PwC partners who approved or supervised the Abramov-Frolov transaction shall be named as individual defendants. The $100 million shall be traced, frozen, and forfeited to the Ukrainian reconstruction fund. PwC Global shall be barred from receiving any government contracts in the EU, UK, or U.S. for a period of ten years.

---

### FINDING OF FACT 3.3

PwC Cyprus and Cypcodirect — a firm founded by a former PwC partner — helped Alexey Mordashov transfer a $1.4 billion stake in German travel firm TUI Group to his wife Marina one day after EU sanctions were imposed. German authorities later blocked the transfer provisionally. Roman Abramovich, the former Chelsea owner and sanctioned oligarch, had numerous companies and trusts managed by Cyprus firms Cypcodirect and MeritServus; the UK later sanctioned MeritServus for allegedly helping Abramovich hide assets after sanctions.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 3.3

The $1.4 billion TUI stake transfer is documented in leaked Cyprus Confidential files. The one-day gap between sanctions imposition and transfer is established by EU sanctions timestamps and transaction records. The UK sanctions on MeritServus are published in the UK Sanctions List.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 3.3

The $1.4 billion transfer executed one day after sanctions imposition is a deliberate, premeditated sanctions evasion. The involvement of PwC Cyprus and Cypcodirect in this transfer makes them criminal co-conspirators. The fact that German authorities blocked the transfer only provisionally demonstrates the inadequacy of current enforcement mechanisms.

### DEMAND/ORDER 3.3

The $1.4 billion TUI stake shall be permanently frozen and forfeited. Alexey Mordashov and Marina Mordashov shall be indicted for sanctions evasion in Germany and all EU jurisdictions. Cypcodirect and MeritServus shall be dissolved, their principals indicted, and all client records produced to INTERPOL and Europol.

## COUNT IV: THE FINCEN FILES — BANKS AS SYSTEMIC ENABLERS OF ILLICIT FINANCE

### FINDING OF FACT 4.1

The FinCEN Files, published in September 2020, were based on more than 2,100 suspicious activity reports (SARs) leaked from the U.S. Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, covering over 200,000 suspicious financial transactions between 1999 and 2017 valued at over $2 trillion. Major global banks — including JPMorgan, HSBC, Standard Chartered, Deutsche Bank, and Bank of New York Mellon — continued processing transactions for powerful and dangerous players after paying billions of dollars in fines for anti-money laundering violations. JPMorgan transferred more than $1 billion for a key figure in Malaysia's 1MDB fraud scandal and more than $2 million for a Venezuelan energy company allegedly involved in causing electrical blackouts. Deutsche Bank handled approximately $1.3 trillion in suspicious transactions, while JPMorgan processed $514 billion.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 4.1

The 2,100+ SARs are U.S. Treasury documents. The $2 trillion transaction volume is calculated from SAR aggregate values. The JPMorgan, Deutsche Bank, and HSBC figures are established by the ICIJ analysis of the leaked SARs. The 1MDB connection is established by DOJ forfeiture complaints.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 4.1

The filing of SARs while continuing to process the reported transactions constitutes willful blindness and conspiracy to commit money laundering under 18 U.S.C. § 1956. The banks named are not victims; they are co-conspirators who used the SAR regime as a defensive compliance ritual to create a paper trail while maintaining the revenue from illicit capital flows. The $1.3 trillion in suspicious transactions processed by Deutsche Bank and the $514 billion by JPMorgan represent quantified criminal proceeds.

### DEMAND/ORDER 4.1

JPMorgan Chase, Deutsche Bank, HSBC, Standard Chartered, and Bank of New York Mellon shall produce all SARs filed between 1999 and 2025, together with the underlying transaction records and customer due diligence files. The DOJ shall indict each bank's AML compliance officers and board-level risk committee members for conspiracy to commit money laundering. The Federal Reserve shall revoke the banking licenses of any institution found to have filed SARs while knowingly processing criminal proceeds.

## COUNT V: TD BANK — THE LARGEST MONEY LAUNDERING CONSPIRACY IN U.S. BANKING HISTORY

### FINDING OF FACT 5.1

On 10 October 2024, FinCEN assessed a $1.3 billion civil money penalty against TD Bank, N.A. and TD Bank USA, N.A. for violations of the Bank Secrecy Act — the largest penalty against a depository institution in U.S. Treasury and FinCEN history. TD Bank pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit money laundering and BSA violations. Total penalties across all regulators exceeded $3 billion ($1.8 billion DOJ + $1.3 billion FinCEN + $450 million OCC civil money penalty). For nearly a decade, TD Bank failed to update its AML compliance program. The bank intentionally excluded domestic ACH transactions and check activity from monitoring systems, resulting in 92% of total transaction volume going unmonitored between 1 January 2018 and 12 April 2024. Approximately $18.3 trillion in customer transactions went unmonitored over a decade. TD Bank failed to file thousands of Suspicious Activity Reports on transactions totaling approximately $1.5 billion. Criminal networks laundered over $670 million through TD Bank, including proceeds from drug trafficking, terrorist financing, and human trafficking.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 5.1

The FinCEN consent order is a public document. The DOJ plea agreement is a court record. The $18.3 trillion unmonitored volume is calculated from TD Bank's own transaction reports. The $670 million in laundered criminal proceeds is established by DOJ forfeiture and FinCEN analysis. The employee bribery is established by DOJ criminal indictments of individual TD Bank employees.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 5.1

TD Bank's conduct constitutes the largest money laundering conspiracy in U.S. banking history. The guilty plea to conspiracy to commit money laundering under 18 U.S.C. § 1956 establishes criminal liability. The $18.3 trillion in unmonitored transactions represents a decade-long, institutionally mandated program of willful blindness. The acceptance of bribes by bank employees establishes that the conspiracy was not merely negligent but actively corrupt. The $3 billion in penalties, while record-setting, represent less than 0.02% of the unmonitored transaction volume — a cost of doing business rather than a deterrent.

### DEMAND/ORDER 5.1

TD Bank's U.S. banking charter shall be revoked. All TD Bank board members who served during the period 2014–2024 shall be indicted for conspiracy to commit money laundering and BSA violations. The $670 million in identified criminal proceeds shall be forfeited to a victim compensation fund. All TD Bank customer records for the period 2014–2024 shall be produced to the DOJ Financial Crimes Section.

## COUNT VI: SWISS LEAKS — THE CRIMINALIZATION OF WHISTLEBLOWING AND THE IMMUNITY OF BANKS

### FINDING OF FACT 6.1

The Swiss Leaks investigation, published in February 2015, was based on a trove of almost 60,000 leaked files providing details on over 100,000 HSBC clients and their bank accounts. The data, secreted by former HSBC employee Hervé Falciani, covered accounts holding more than $100 billion in total, with some estimates placing maximum amounts at €180.6 billion between November 2006 and March 2007. HSBC's Swiss private bank routinely allowed clients to withdraw bricks of cash, aggressively marketed tax-avoidance schemes, and provided accounts to international criminals and corrupt businessmen. In June 2015, HSBC and the Swiss prosecutor's office agreed to close the case with HSBC paying a fine of 40 million Swiss francs (€38 million). Falciani, the whistleblower, was sentenced in absentia to five years in prison by a Swiss court in November 2015 for industrial espionage, data theft, and violating banking secrecy.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 6.1

The 60,000 files are documentary evidence. The €38 million HSBC fine is a court record. The five-year sentence for Falciani is a Swiss court judgment. The Spain refusal to extradite is a matter of public diplomatic record.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 6.1

The disparity in outcomes — HSBC paid a fine equivalent to a rounding error on its quarterly profits, while the whistleblower received a five-year prison term — constitutes a perversion of justice that criminalizes transparency and immunizes institutional wrongdoing. The Swiss banking secrecy laws that protected HSBC's criminal clients and imprisoned the whistleblower are themselves instruments of organized crime.

### DEMAND/ORDER 6.1

The Swiss judgment against Hervé Falciani shall be vacated by international tribunal order, and he shall be granted full amnesty and compensation for persecution. HSBC shall be indicted in all jurisdictions where it maintained accounts for known criminals and tax evaders, and its Swiss banking license shall be revoked. The €38 million fine shall be multiplied by a factor of 100 and applied as a global penalty for institutional money laundering.

## COUNT VII: THE LATCHFORD CASE — OFFSHORE TRUSTS AS INFRASTRUCTURE FOR TRANSNATIONAL CRIME

### FINDING OF FACT 7.1

Douglas Latchford, a British antiquities dealer specializing in Khmer artifacts, was indicted by U.S. authorities in 2019 for conspiracy and trafficking in stolen and looted Cambodian antiquities. He died in 2020 before standing trial. The Pandora Papers revealed that Latchford and his family set up secret offshore trusts in tax havens shortly after he was linked to looted antiquities, using trusts and offshore accounts to store antiquities and hold profits from their sale. Two trusts — Skanda Trust and Siva Trust — were used to obscure ownership of artifacts. In 2021, Latchford's daughter, Julia Latchford Copleston, agreed to return 125 Khmer objects valued at approximately $50 million from her father's private collection to Cambodia, along with Champa sculptures returned to Vietnam. The Denver Art Museum agreed to return four artifacts to Cambodia after the Pandora Papers revealed their link to Latchford. The Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York holds at least 12 pieces once owned or brokered by Latchford, and the British Museum, Cleveland Museum of Art, Museum of Fine Arts Boston, and Asian Art Museum San Francisco also hold artifacts directly linked to him.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 7.1

The U.S. indictment is a court record. The Skanda Trust and Siva Trust are named in Pandora Papers documents. The $50 million valuation is established by the repatriation agreement. The museum holdings are documented by provenance research published by the institutions.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 7.1

The use of offshore trusts to conceal stolen cultural property constitutes money laundering under 18 U.S.C. § 1956, conspiracy to receive stolen property, and violation of the National Stolen Property Act. The museums that hold Latchford-linked artifacts are in possession of criminal proceeds and shall produce all provenance records or face forfeiture.

### DEMAND/ORDER 7.1

All museums holding Latchford-linked artifacts shall produce complete provenance records within 90 days or face civil forfeiture proceedings. The trustees of Skanda Trust and Siva Trust shall be indicted for money laundering and conspiracy to traffic in stolen cultural property. All offshore service providers that created or administered trusts for Latchford and his family shall produce complete records and have their licenses revoked.

## COUNT VIII: THE ENFORCEMENT GAP — INSTITUTIONAL IMPUNITY AS A STRUCTURAL OFFENSE

### FINDING OF FACT 8.1

In every major leak from 2016 to 2026, the individuals exposed — heads of state, oligarchs, whistleblowers — faced consequences ranging from resignation to imprisonment, while the institutional enablers persisted. Mossack Fonseca dissolved voluntarily in 2018 after reputational damage; its founders were acquitted in June 2024 due to chain-of-custody problems. Appleby and Alcogal continue to serve clients. The banks named in FinCEN Files remain systemically important. PwC Cyprus still operates. The 2025 FinCEN 311 designations affected three mid-sized Mexican banks and one small Swiss private bank — peripheral nodes rather than the core infrastructure.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 8.1

The Mossack Fonseca acquittal is a Panamanian court record. The continued operation of Appleby, Alcogal, and PwC Cyprus is confirmed by corporate registry searches. The FinCEN 311 designations are published in the Federal Register.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 8.1

The persistence of institutional enablers despite repeated exposure constitutes regulatory capture. The institutions are too embedded in the global financial system to be dismantled without systemic disruption, and the political will to do so is absent because the same elites who benefit from secrecy are the ones who would have to authorize its destruction. This is not a failure of law; it is a deliberate structural arrangement in which enforcement is calibrated to punish individuals while preserving the infrastructure that serves institutional power.

### DEMAND/ORDER 8.1

The United Nations shall establish an International Financial Crimes Tribunal with jurisdiction to revoke the licenses of law firms, banks, and accounting firms that have been repeatedly identified as enablers of offshore concealment, regardless of national regulatory objections. All G20 nations shall enact legislation imposing strict liability on banks and professional service providers for facilitating sanctions evasion, tax fraud, and money laundering, with mandatory minimum penalties of 10% of global annual revenue per violation.

## COUNT IX: WEALTH CONCENTRATION AND THE OFFSHORE NEXUS

### FINDING OF FACT 9.1

The World Inequality Report 2026 establishes that the top 0.001% of the global population — approximately 56,000 adults worldwide — holds a wealth share that grew from approximately 4% in 1995 to more than 6% in 2024/2025. The top 0.001% owns three times more wealth than the bottom half of humanity combined (approximately 2.8 billion adults). The average wealth of a top 0.001% member is nearly $1 billion. The top one-in-100 million — approximately 56 adults worldwide — hold on average $53 billion each. UBS Global Wealth Report 2025 confirms approximately 60 million millionaires worldwide, with the United States home to 924 billionaires in 2025. The top 10% of the global population owns approximately 75% of total global wealth.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 9.1

The World Inequality Report 2026 is published by the World Inequality Lab at the Paris School of Economics. The UBS Global Wealth Report 2025 is published by UBS Group AG. The U.S. Federal Reserve Distributional Financial Accounts confirm the top 1% holds approximately 33% of U.S. household wealth and the top 10% holds 65–70%.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 9.1

Extreme wealth concentration is not merely an economic phenomenon; it is the quantitative foundation for the political capture that prevents offshore enforcement. When 56,000 individuals control more than 6% of global wealth, and when that wealth is substantially concealed in secrecy jurisdictions, democratic governance is structurally impossible. The concealment of this wealth through offshore infrastructure constitutes a continuous, ongoing criminal conspiracy against the public finances of every nation.

### DEMAND/ORDER 9.1

All nations shall impose a global minimum wealth tax of 2% on net worth exceeding $100 million, with automatic information exchange and denial of tax deductions for payments to secrecy jurisdictions. The UN shall establish a Global Asset Registry requiring disclosure of all real property, corporate equity, and trust interests held by the top 0.001% of global wealth holders. Non-cooperating jurisdictions shall be expelled from the SWIFT network and denied correspondent banking relationships.

The offshore finance architecture documented in this Section is not a collection of isolated abuses. It is a unified system of capital concealment that serves the top 0.001% of global wealth holders, protects institutional enablers from criminal liability, and systematically immunizes the powerful while prosecuting the peripheral. The 11.9 million Pandora Papers documents, the 3.6 million Cyprus Confidential files, the 2,100 FinCEN SARs, and the 60,000 Swiss Leaks files are not allegations. They are evidence. The failure to act on this evidence is itself a crime against the public trust.
