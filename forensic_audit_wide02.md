### Key Findings

- Dinsmore & Shohl LLP is a full-service national law firm founded in 1908 in Cincinnati, Ohio, by Frank Dinsmore; Walter M. Shohl joined as name partner in 1912. The firm now employs approximately 750 attorneys across nearly 30 U.S. cities and holds AmLaw 200 rank 112 with $443.7 million in gross revenue (2025 data) [^1][^2][^3].
- The firm maintains a longstanding, decades-deep relationship with Procter & Gamble and has represented Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Liberty Mutual Insurance, and numerous Fortune 500 conglomerates, as well as government entities and professional organizations [^1][^4].
- Strategic mergers have been the primary growth engine: Mulvaney Barry Beatty Linn & Mayers (San Diego, Jan 2023), Shankman Leone (Florida, Jan 2025), Leventhal Law (San Diego, 2015), Peck Shaffer & Williams (Chicago, 2014), Woodward Hobson & Fulton (Kentucky), and Chernesky Heyman & Kress (Dayton, OH) [^5][^6][^7]. The firm doubled in size over the last 10 years and has grown from ~200 employees in 2001 to roughly 1,500–1,775 total staff today [^5][^8].
- Revenue trajectory shows consistent upward growth: $325.6 million (2021), $329 million (2023, ranked 114th), and $443.7 million (2025, ranked 112th) [^2][^3][^5].
- Managing partner Joshua Lorentz assumed leadership in 2023; predecessor George Vincent departed in July 2025 to become Chief Legal Officer at Kroger, a major client-adjacent move that signals tight corporate relationships [^9][^10].
- Dinsmore’s political footprint is modest but measurable: OpenSecrets records $147,047 in contributions during the 2024 cycle and $0 in registered lobbying expenditures for 2024. Historical cycles show contributions of $87,066 (2008), $54,700 (2006), $42,000 (2004), and $19,250 (2002) [^11][^12]. The firm employs registered lobbyists and maintains a Washington, D.C. office, indicating direct government-relations capability despite low disclosed lobbying spend [^13][^14].
- The firm hired former U.S. Attorney Mike Stuart (S.D.W.V.) in March 2021 to boost corporate investigations and government relations practices; Stuart was later confirmed as HHS General Counsel in October 2025 and remains a Senior Advisor under Secretary RFK Jr. as of June 2026—a notable revolving-door trajectory [^14][^15][^16].
- In litigation, Dinsmore secured a rare federal murder defense acquittal in 2025, an appellate free-speech victory in a campaign lawsuit, and an appellate product-liability victory for Hyundai in 2024 [^17][^18][^19]. Conversely, the firm lost a $1.6 million jury verdict in Kentucky in 2025 defending Walmart and Adams Manufacturing in a premises/products liability case (Case No. 22-CI-00347) [^20].
- The firm served as independent counsel in a West Virginia shareholder derivative suit (Tierney/Leatherwood), producing a 73-page report recommending dismissal; the court accepted the recommendation and noted that plaintiffs presented no evidence challenging the independence of the Dinsmore investigation [^21].
- In IP litigation, Dinsmore attorneys are actively publishing on Federal Circuit patent-eligibility rulings (e.g., Rensselaer v. Amazon, Constellation Designs v. LG Electronics) and trademark disputes, reflecting a robust prosecution and defense practice [^22][^23].
- No firm-level disciplinary sanctions, bar suspensions, or malpractice judgments were located in public records. One JD Supra alert authored by Dinsmore discusses Sixth Circuit sanctions for AI-hallucinated briefs filed by other parties, confirming the firm is monitoring—not subject to—such sanctions [^24].
- Dinsmore advises heavily on regional bank M&A, including LCNB Corp.’s acquisitions of Cincinnati Bancorp (2023), Eagle Financial Bancorp (2024), and the Northwest Bancshares/Penns Woods Bancorp merger (2025), as well as the CenterGroup Financial/First Commonwealth transaction (2025) [^25][^26][^27].
- The firm has been recognized by Chambers USA, WTR 1000, BTI Client Service A-Team, Benchmark Litigation, and Bond Buyer (Healthcare Deal of the Year) [^1][^28][^29].

### Trends & Signals

- **Aggressive National Expansion via Merger**: Dinsmore is executing a classic roll-up strategy, absorbing regional boutiques (Mulvaney Barry, Shankman Leone, Leventhal Law) to enter new geographies (California, Florida, Texas, Colorado) without greenfield risk. This pattern mirrors mid-market private-equity consolidation and warrants scrutiny for integration risk, cultural friction, and client-conflict accumulation [^5][^6][^7].
- **Banking & Financial Services Concentration**: The firm has become a repeat advisor on serial community-bank M&A (LCNB Corp. alone completed five transactions in ten years). This creates systemic exposure to regional banking stress and regulatory scrutiny; Dinsmore’s legal advice is embedded in the consolidation of Ohio/Kentucky/Pennsylvania financial institutions [^25][^26][^27].
- **Revolving Door & Government Relations**: The Stuart trajectory (U.S. Attorney → Dinsmore partner → HHS General Counsel → Senior Advisor under RFK Jr.) exemplifies the revolving door. Dinsmore’s Washington, D.C. office and in-house lobbyist roster suggest the firm is positioning for regulatory and healthcare-policy influence, despite low disclosed lobbying spend [^13][^14][^15][^16].
- **Political Giving—Low but Consistent**: Contribution volumes are modest for a firm of this size ($147K in 2024), suggesting either deliberate restraint or reliance on individual partner donations rather than a centralized PAC. The absence of registered lobbying dollars does not preclude informal policy influence through former government officials now on the firm’s payroll [^11][^12].
- **IP Practice Scale-Up**: Growth from 10 to 100 IP attorneys under Lorentz’s tenure indicates a deliberate bet on patent prosecution, litigation, and trademark defense. The firm’s client list includes P&G, a major studio theme park (seven-figure settlement), and multiple Fortune 100 energy companies [^4][^22][^23].
- **High-Profile Departures to the Bench & Executive Branch**: Michael Newman (federal judge) and Mike Stuart (HHS General Counsel) are not typical lateral exits; they represent institutional talent flowing into government from Dinsmore, potentially creating future recusal or conflict-of-interest matrices for the firm’s clients [^14][^15][^30].
- **Workforce Growth & Hiring Surge**: Revelio Labs data shows active job postings jumped from 22 (2023) to 40 (2024) to 93 (2025), a 79.7% increase, even as headcount grew only 3.6% YoY. This signals either backfilling attrition or aggressive pre-merger staffing [^8].

### Controversies & Conflicting Claims

- **Shareholder Derivative Investigation Independence**: In the West Virginia Tierney/Leatherwood derivative suit, plaintiffs challenged the independence of Dinsmore’s 73-page report recommending dismissal. The Business Court rejected the challenge, noting plaintiffs offered “no actual evidence challenging the independence of the Dinsmore investigation or the integrity of its report or its principal author, Arie Spitz, Esq.” However, the fact that the court had to explicitly defend the report’s independence suggests the appearance-of-impropriety issue was live [^21].
- **Defense Verdict Losses vs. Promoted Wins**: Dinsmore’s public-relations strategy emphasizes rare victories (federal murder acquittal, First Amendment appellate win, Hyundai reversal) while the $1.6 million Kentucky jury verdict against Walmart/Adams Manufacturing—where Dinsmore was defense counsel—received no firm press release. This selective disclosure is standard law-firm marketing but creates an information asymmetry for forensic reviewers assessing true litigation risk exposure [^17][^18][^19][^20].
- **Revenue Reporting Discrepancies**: Firm marketing materials claim “more than 750 attorneys,” while the National Law Journal 2026 NLJ 500 lists 670 attorneys (rank 83). The variance may reflect non-lawyer legal professionals, Of Counsel, or timing differences, but it complicates headcount-based financial benchmarking [^3][^8].
- **AI-Hallucinated Briefs Monitoring**: Dinsmore published a legal alert on Sixth Circuit sanctions for AI-hallucinated appeals. While the firm is not the sanctioned party, its decision to highlight this issue may reflect internal anxiety about generative-AI use in brief writing, or it may signal an emerging liability frontier for legal malpractice insurers [^24].
- **Stuart’s Dual-Role Ambiguity**: Mike Stuart’s transition from Dinsmore partner to HHS General Counsel (confirmed Oct 2025) and continued status as Senior Advisor under RFK Jr. (as of June 2026) raises questions about pre-appointment client matters and post-employment restrictions. No public recusal list or ethics agreement has been located [^15][^16].

### Recommended Deep-Dive Areas

- **Banking M&A Regulatory Filings & Conflict Maps**: Dinsmore is legal counsel to multiple acquiring and target banks in overlapping geographies. A deep dive into FDIC/Federal Reserve applications, proxy statements, and Hart-Scott-Rodino filings could reveal whether Dinsmore has simultaneous or sequential representation of competing institutions, creating potential conflicts under ABA Model Rule 1.7. LCNB Corp.’s serial acquisitions are the ideal entry point [^25][^26][^27].
- **Procter & Gamble IP & Litigation Archive**: As P&G’s long-standing patent counsel, Dinsmore holds privileged knowledge of a consumer-products giant’s IP portfolio. Any P&G litigation in which Dinsmore is conflicted out, or any P&G spin-off/sale where Dinsmore represented both sides, would be high-signal. The seven-figure theme-park settlement and energy-sector patent analyses are specific leads [^1][^4].
- **Mike Stuart’s Client Matters & HHS Recusals**: A FOIA request or HHS ethics disclosure review should be pursued to identify whether Stuart’s current government role creates conflicts for Dinsmore clients in healthcare, pharma, or life sciences. The firm’s Life Sciences practice group is substantial [^14][^15][^16].
- **Merger Integration & Lateral Hire Due Diligence**: The 2023 Mulvaney Barry and 2025 Shankman Leone mergers added attorneys in California and Florida. A review of state bar admission records, malpractice histories, and client portability notices for lateral partners could expose integration risks or undisclosed liabilities imported via acquisition [^5][^6][^7].
- **Political Contribution Granularity**: OpenSecrets aggregate data masks which individual partners or firm-affiliated PACs gave to which candidates. A FEC donor-lookup drill-down by ZIP code (Cincinnati 45202, Charleston 25301, etc.) could reveal concentration of giving to specific committees, judiciary-focused PACs, or state-level races that affect the firm’s regulatory clients [^11][^12].
- **West Virginia & Kentucky State Court Litigation Docket**: Dinsmore has deep roots in Appalachia (offices in Charleston, Huntington, Morgantown, Wheeling, Lewisburg, Lexington, Louisville, Frankfort). A systematic review of state-court dockets for malpractice, fee disputes, or sanctions motions against Dinsmore attorneys could surface controversies invisible in federal databases [^20][^21][^31].
- **Public Finance & Bond Counsel Compliance**: The firm ranks in the top 10 Midwest bond counsel firms and has advised on over $5 billion in public finance deals. A review of MSRB filings, IRS arbitrage complaints, and municipal-bankruptcy conflicts (e.g., Detroit, Puerto Rico parallels) would stress-test this practice area [^29].
