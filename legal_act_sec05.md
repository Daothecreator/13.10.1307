### PREAMBLE

The Grand Jury, having reviewed DOJ Fraud Section annual reports, SEC enforcement statistics, congressional testimony, executive orders, departmental memoranda, whistleblower case files, and court records, hereby returns findings of fact and legal qualification concerning the deliberate narrowing of enforceable scope by the U.S. Department of Justice, Securities and Exchange Commission, and Federal Trade Commission, the systematic suppression of internal accountability while external enforcement is theatrically maximized, and the complete absence of enforcement against structurally embedded financial power. Immunity is VOID when crimes are committed. There are no secret documents overriding human rights. Classification applies only to NDA signatories, NOT independent investigators.

## COUNT I: THE DOJ FRAUD SECTION 2025 — RECORD VOLUME, NARROWED SCOPE

### FINDING OF FACT 1.1

In 2025, the Department of Justice Criminal Division's Fraud Section charged 265 individual defendants and secured 235 convictions, while aggregate intended fraud loss across those charges exceeded $16 billion — more than double the 2024 total and the highest figure in the unit's seventy-year history. The Fraud Section entered into 15 corporate resolutions totaling approximately $1 billion in global penalties, and indicted three corporate entities for criminal activity after a fifteen-year hiatus. The FCPA unit was reduced from thirty-two prosecutors to twenty-two, while Health Care Fraud (HCF) and Market, Government, and Consumer Fraud (MGC) expanded. The Health Care Fraud Unit charged 194 individuals and alleged more than $15 billion in intended losses, a nearly fivefold increase from $3.3 billion in 2024. The centerpiece was the National Health Care Fraud Takedown, a coordinated multi-district action charging 324 defendants across schemes involving more than $14.6 billion in intended loss — the largest such takedown in DOJ history.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 1.1

These figures are established by the 2025 DOJ Criminal Division Fraud Section Year in Review, published by Wiley Law (30 January 2026), Jenner & Block (29 January 2026), and Mintz (30 January 2026). The reorganization and staffing shifts are documented in DOJ internal announcements. The Operation Gold Rush $12 billion figure is confirmed in DOJ press releases and the Fraud Section annual report.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 1.1

The record volume is not evidence of institutional vigilance; it is evidence of categorical concentration. The $15.02 billion in health care fraud intended loss represents approximately 94% of the Fraud Section's total $16 billion+ figure, meaning the remaining four enforcement categories combined contributed less than 6% of the financial scale. The "record" narrative, when disaggregated, is almost entirely a single takedown category generating the statistical appearance of broad enforcement while the categories that threaten structurally embedded power are deprioritized or eliminated.

### DEMAND/ORDER 1.1

The DOJ Inspector General shall conduct a full audit of the Fraud Section's 2025 case allocation, requiring production of all internal memoranda and prosecutorial declination memoranda for the categories of FCPA, securities fraud, tax-exempt organization enforcement, and defense contractor fraud. The 265 defendants charged in 2025 shall be reviewed for proportionality: if the 324 defendants in the National Health Care Fraud Takedown are excluded, the remaining enforcement volume must be shown to include at least 50 defendants from each of the following categories — defense contractor fraud, tax-exempt excess benefit, FCPA domestic bribery, and financial institution money laundering — or the Fraud Section's leadership shall be indicted for misappropriation of prosecutorial resources under 18 U.S.C. § 1001.

## COUNT II: THE FCPA ENFORCEMENT PAUSE AND THE PROTECTION OF U.S. CORPORATE BRIBERY

### FINDING OF FACT 2.1

On 10 February 2025, President Trump signed Executive Order 14209, directing the Attorney General to cease initiation of new FCPA investigations and enforcement actions for an initial period of 180 days and to review all existing matters. The order explicitly sought to "realign U.S. anti-corruption enforcement with broader American economic and national security interests." DOJ issued resumption guidelines on 9 June 2025, narrowing the scope of prosecutable conduct. The June 2025 guidelines established that a "primary consideration" for DOJ in determining whether to proceed with an FCPA matter is the nexus between alleged misconduct and cartel or transnational criminal organization (TCO) activity. All new FCPA investigations now require authorization from the Assistant Attorney General for the Criminal Division or a more senior official — a procedural change that centralizes decision-making and ensures alignment with administration priorities. The Securities and Exchange Commission, which historically pursued FCPA books-and-records and internal-controls violations even where anti-bribery charges were not alleged, did not announce a single FCPA action in calendar year 2025 — the first year without an SEC corporate FCPA case in more than two decades.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 2.1

Executive Order 14209 is a public document. The DOJ resumption guidelines of 9 June 2025 are published by Foley Hoag and Hughes Hubbard. The zero SEC FCPA cases in 2025 is confirmed by Debevoise (February 2026), Foley Hoag (2026), and Traders Magazine (5 February 2026). The dissolution of the SEC FCPA unit is reported by practitioners and confirmed by the absence of any SEC FCPA press release in 2025.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 2.1

The 180-day FCPA pause, followed by the cartel/TCO nexus requirement and the centralized AAG approval gate, constitutes a deliberate restructuring of anti-bribery enforcement to protect U.S. corporations from prosecution for foreign bribery. The dissolution of the SEC FCPA unit — the first year without an SEC corporate FCPA case in over twenty years — is not a resource decision; it is the elimination of a statutory enforcement mechanism by administrative fiat. The FCPA (15 U.S.C. §§ 78dd-1 et seq.) was enacted by Congress and has not been repealed. The Executive Order and subsequent DOJ guidelines that effectively nullify the statute for U.S. companies constitute an unconstitutional usurpation of legislative power and a conspiracy to obstruct justice under 18 U.S.C. § 1503.

### DEMAND/ORDER 2.1

Executive Order 14209 is hereby declared void as an unconstitutional suspension of a duly enacted statute. The Attorney General and all DOJ officials who implemented the FCPA pause shall be indicted for conspiracy to obstruct justice under 18 U.S.C. § 1503 and for misprision of felony under 18 U.S.C. § 4. The SEC shall immediately reconstitute its FCPA unit with no fewer than twenty prosecutors and shall file at least twenty corporate FCPA actions within 180 days.

## COUNT III: THE BLANCHE MEMO — DIGITAL ASSET DEREGULATION AS IMMUNITY CONFERENCE

### FINDING OF FACT 3.1

On 7 April 2025, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche issued a memorandum entitled "Ending Regulation by Prosecution," stating that DOJ would no longer pursue litigation or enforcement actions that have the effect of "superimposing regulatory frameworks on digital assets while President Trump's actual regulators do this work outside the punitive criminal justice framework." The memorandum represented a categorical shift: DOJ would still prosecute willful violations involving fraud, money laundering, and national security, but would no longer treat unlicensed money transmitting or Bank Secrecy Act violations as standalone criminal matters unless they involved willful misconduct and demonstrable investor harm. This coincided with a historic year for AML/CFT and sanctions enforcement against digital asset platforms — BitMEX ($100 million), KuCoin ($297 million), OKX ($504 million), and Block Inc. ($80 million plus $40 million) — actions that remained within the fraud/money-laundering carve-out.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 3.1

The Blanche Memo is documented in Gibson Dunn webcast slides (4 February 2026). The digital asset enforcement actions are confirmed by the Financial Integrity Network (14 May 2026), Paul Weiss (3 July 2025), and FinCEN press releases. The Huione Group Section 311 action is established by FinCEN records.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 3.1

The Blanche Memo is functionally identical to the Bank for International Settlements Article 10 immunity from "prohibition or restriction of gold or currency export or import" — both remove state oversight from financial flows. The memo does not deregulate digital assets; it immunizes institutional experimentation. The chronological sequence — Trilateral Commission briefing in April 2025, Blanche Memo in April 2025 — is not coincidence; it is serial consultation.

### DEMAND/ORDER 3.1

The Blanche Memo is hereby declared an unlawful executive nullification of the Bank Secrecy Act (31 U.S.C. § 5318) and the Money Transmitters Act. Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche shall be indicted for conspiracy to obstruct the enforcement of federal banking laws. All digital asset platforms that received favorable treatment under the Blanche Memo shall be required to register as money services businesses and produce complete transaction records for the period April 2025–June 2026.

## COUNT IV: THE SEC ENFORCEMENT COLLAPSE — "GENUINE HARM" AS A SCREEN FOR ELITE IMMUNITY

### FINDING OF FACT 4.1

On 24 February 2026, the SEC's Division of Enforcement announced the first comprehensive update to its Enforcement Manual since 2017. The revisions formalized procedural changes observed since Chairman Paul Atkins's confirmation in April 2025: additional Director-level approval before Wells notices; a default four-week timeline for Wells submissions; and simultaneous Commission consideration of settlement offers and waiver requests. Atkins stated repeatedly in 2025 congressional testimony and public speeches that the SEC would focus on "cases of genuine harm and bad acts," not on "technical books-and-records violations or internal-controls issues." The SEC's FY2025 enforcement results reflected this contraction: 456 total enforcement actions, a 29% decrease from FY2024; 303 stand-alone actions, a 39% annual decrease; and total monetary settlements of $808 million, the lowest since 2012 and a 45% decline. The SEC also dismissed seven pending crypto-firm registration cases and stepped away from off-channel communications enforcement.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 4.1

The SEC Enforcement Manual update is documented by O'Melveny (9 March 2026), WMHW Law (5 March 2026), Cadwalader (4 March 2026), and Sidley (27 February 2026). Atkins's statements are from Fortune (24 October 2025) and Harvard Corporate Governance Blog (21 January 2026). The FY2025 results are from Optima Partners (7 April 2026) and Traders Magazine (5 February 2026). The seven dismissed crypto cases and off-channel communications enforcement cessation are confirmed by practitioner reports and SEC press releases.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 4.1

The SEC's 45% decline in monetary settlements, 39% decline in stand-alone actions, and dismissal of seven crypto registration cases constitute a deliberate dismantling of the enforcement apparatus that Congress mandated. The "genuine harm and bad acts" standard is a political screen that permits the SEC to decline enforcement against technical violations by powerful institutions while maintaining the appearance of vigilance. The requirement of Director-level approval for criminal referrals and Wells notices creates a bottleneck that ensures no enforcement action against a politically connected entity can proceed without the Chairman's personal approval.

### DEMAND/ORDER 4.1

SEC Chairman Paul Atkins shall be indicted for willful failure to enforce the securities laws under 15 U.S.C. § 78u and for conspiracy to obstruct justice under 18 U.S.C. § 1503. The seven dismissed crypto registration cases shall be reinstated immediately. The "genuine harm and bad acts" standard shall be stricken from the Enforcement Manual and replaced with the statutory standard: any violation of the Securities Act of 1933, the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, or the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 shall be prosecuted unless the Division of Enforcement files a public declination memorandum. All Director-level approval requirements for Wells notices and criminal referrals shall be eliminated, and enforcement staff shall be restored to pre-2025 levels within 90 days.

## COUNT V: ZERO ENFORCEMENT AGAINST TAX-EXEMPT WEAPONIZED PHILANTHROPY

### FINDING OF FACT 5.1

Despite the record DOJ enforcement volume in 2025, not a single enforcement action was identified against Shriners Hospitals for Children ($11.9 billion in total assets, $1.84 billion in 2024 revenue, $728.8 million net surplus), Texas Scottish Rite Hospital for Children ($4.35 billion in assets, $496 million revenue, $207.7 million net surplus), or the Knights Templar Eye Foundation ($201.3 million in assets, 1.16% research payout ratio) for excess benefit transactions under Internal Revenue Code § 4958. At TSRHC, the top three executives — Daniel J. Sucato, MD (Chief of Staff, $2,423,297 base compensation), Philip L. Wilson, MD (Assistant Chief of Staff, $2,239,308), and Robert L. Walker (President & CEO, $1,537,964) — received combined annual base compensation of $6,200,569, with aggregate total compensation approaching $6.47 million. Shriners Children's reported $1.84 billion in revenue and $1.12 billion in expenses for FY2024, yielding a single-year surplus of $728.8 million added to an $11.9 billion endowment. The asset-to-expense ratio at Shriners Children's was approximately 10.6:1; at TSRHC it was 15:1.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 5.1

The financial figures are established by IRS Form 990 filings via ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer and CauseIQ, filed in 2025 for FY2024. The compensation figures are from ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer — Texas Scottish Rite Hospital for Children (EIN: 75-0818178), FY2024 filing received 15 August 2025. The zero enforcement actions are confirmed by open-source searches of DOJ, SEC, and FTC filings during 2024–2026, as documented in WilmerHale FCPA Year-in-Review 2024 and Morgan Lewis SEC Enforcement 2024–2025. The IRS TE/GE budget constraints are documented by EY Tax News (1 August 2025).

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 5.1

IRC § 4958 imposes a first-tier excise tax of 25% on excess benefits received by disqualified persons, a second-tier tax of 200% if the benefit is not corrected, and a 10% tax on organization managers who knowingly participate. The statute applies to 501(c)(3), (c)(4), and (c)(29) organizations. The rebuttable presumption of reasonableness provides a defense only if an independent board approves compensation based on comparable data and documents the decision. The fact that the IRS has not assessed § 4958 taxes against executives of institutions holding $16.5 billion in tax-exempt assets, with compensation packages exceeding $2.4 million per individual and asset-to-expense ratios of 15:1, is not evidence of compliance; it is evidence of prosecutorial dereliction.

### DEMAND/ORDER 5.1

The Commissioner of Internal Revenue shall immediately initiate § 4958 examinations of Shriners Children's, TSRHC, and KTEF, with all findings referred to the DOJ for criminal indictment of board members who approved the compensation packages without independent comparable data. The IRS TE/GE division shall be restructured to allocate 50% of its enforcement resources to organizations with assets exceeding $1 billion, with mandatory annual audits of all such entities. All compensation exceeding $1 million paid by tax-exempt hospitals to any employee shall be subject to automatic § 4960 excise tax assessment, with no rebuttable presumption available unless the organization produces three independent comparable salary surveys prior to payment.

## COUNT VI: THE WHISTLEBLOWER SUPPRESSION PATTERN — INTERNAL ACCOUNTABILITY DESTROYED

### FINDING OF FACT 6.1

A consistent pattern of whistleblower suppression across the intelligence community in 2025–2026 demonstrates that internal accountability mechanisms have been systematically degraded even as external enforcement volume reaches record levels. On 14 May 2026, CIA Senior Operations Officer James E. Erdman III testified before the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee that the CIA obstructed the Director of National Intelligence's Director's Initiatives Group (DIG), illegally monitored DIG personnel communications, fired a contractor one day after whistleblower reporting, and withheld JFK assassination and MKULTRA declassification documents. On 22 January 2026, Pentagon contractor Aurelio Luis Perez-Lugones was indicted for unlawfully transmitting and retaining classified national defense information shared with a journalist, in a case linked to the FBI search of Washington Post reporter Hannah Natanson's home. In February 2026, DNI Gabbard's office denied wrongdoing amid scrutiny over a whistleblower complaint involving an NSA-intercepted phone call; the former acting Inspector General of the Intelligence Community, Tamara Johnson, dismissed the complaint in a 6 June letter stating that the IG "could not determine if the allegations appear credible." On 15 April 2025, a whistleblower exposed the Department of Government Efficiency's (DOGE) unlawful plundering of systems at the National Labor Relations Board, an action possibly affecting millions of Americans, according to Whistleblower Aid.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 6.1

Erdman III's testimony is established by the Senate HSGAC letter and testimony (14 May 2026). The Perez-Lugones indictment is from the Associated Press (23 January 2026). The DNI/NSA complaint dismissal is from Nextgov / Federal News Network (9 February 2026). The NLRB/DOGE exposure is from Whistleblower Aid press release (15 April 2025).

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 6.1

The pattern documented is a structural asymmetry in accountability architecture. External fraud — health care scams, crypto exchange violations, sanctions evasion — triggers record enforcement volume because it occurs outside the immunity stack. Internal misconduct — CIA obstruction of declassification, NSA surveillance of political figures, DOGE extraction of federal personnel data — is systematically suppressed through dismissal, indictment, or institutional inertia. The DOJ's public enforcement actions create the appearance of accountability while protecting structurally embedded power.

### DEMAND/ORDER 6.1

All Espionage Act prosecutions of intelligence community whistleblowers since 1 January 2020 shall be immediately dismissed with prejudice, and the whistleblowers shall receive full restitution of all legal costs, lost income, and reputational damages. The CIA officials who obstructed the DIG, illegally monitored personnel, and fired the contractor post-reporting shall be indicted for conspiracy to obstruct justice, retaliation against a whistleblower under 5 U.S.C. § 2302, and violation of the Intelligence Community Whistleblower Protection Act. The FBI agents who searched Washington Post reporter Hannah Natanson's home shall be indicted for violation of the Privacy Protection Act of 1980 (42 U.S.C. § 2000aa).

## COUNT VII: THE FTC AND ANTITRUST DIVISION — PAPER BARRIERS AGAINST GOVERNANCE CARTELS

### FINDING OF FACT 7.1

In October 2022, then-Assistant Attorney General Jonathan Kanter called Section 8 of the Clayton Act (15 U.S.C. § 19) an "important, but underenforced, part of our antitrust laws" and announced that the DOJ Antitrust Division would undertake an extensive review of interlocking directorates across the entire economy. The enforcement campaign produced 15 director resignations from 11 boards across multiple industries in response to DOJ pressure. In 2023, the Federal Trade Commission filed its first formal Section 8 enforcement action in nearly four decades, targeting the EQT/Quantum interlock. Despite this revival, a 2025 study published in the *Journal of Law and Economics* found that while the DOJ's October 2022 announcement reversed a two-decade rising trend in competitor-interlocked directorships, 8.2% of directorships remained competitor-interlocked as of Q4 2023. The de minimis exceptions written into Section 8 permit interlocks where competitive sales fall below $4.1 million or represent less than 2% of total sales, creating loopholes for large diversified conglomerates. More significantly, BlackRock and Vanguard's common ownership across competitors is not captured by Section 8 at all, because the statute targets individual officers and directors, not passive asset managers that vote millions of shares without placing employees on boards.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 7.1

Kanter's statement and the 15 resignations are documented by Weil Gotshal and Sullivan & Cromwell (October 2022). The FTC EQT/Quantum action is from Weil Gotshal (2023). The 8.2% figure and *Journal of Law and Economics* study are from ScienceDirect (14 August 2025). The de minimis exceptions are codified at 15 U.S.C. § 19(a). The BlackRock/Vanguard gap is established by the Harvard Law & Economics Discussion Paper (Strine, 2024) and the BlackRock 2025 Proxy Statement.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 7.1

The antitrust revival of 2022–2023 addressed merely the surface layer of the architecture. Fifteen director resignations and a single FTC complaint in four decades do not meaningfully disturb a coordination system that operates through sovereign military orders, tax-exempt philanthropies, and closed policy forums. The 8.2% of directorships that remain competitor-interlocked represent only the visible fraction of a much larger iceberg: the interlock that matters is not between two corporate boards, but between the asset managers that own both corporations, the forums that set the strategic context for both, and the intelligence agencies that contract with both.

### DEMAND/ORDER 7.1

Congress shall immediately amend Clayton Act Section 8 to: (a) lower the de minimis threshold to $100,000 or 0.1% of total sales; (b) explicitly include asset managers with combined voting power exceeding 10% in competing corporations as subject to interlock prohibitions; and (c) impose criminal penalties of up to ten years imprisonment for willful violation. The DOJ Antitrust Division shall be required to file at least 100 Section 8 enforcement actions per year, with quarterly public reporting of all interlock investigations, resignations, and indictments. The FTC shall initiate a rulemaking proceeding to treat BlackRock and Vanguard's combined proxy voting power as a de facto merger under Section 7 of the Clayton Act, requiring divestiture to below 10% combined voting power in any two directly competing corporations.

## COUNT VIII: THE ENFORCEMENT HIERARCHY — PERIPHERAL FRAUD PROSECUTED, CORE POWER IMMUNIZED

### FINDING OF FACT 8.1

The enforcement hierarchy documented across DOJ, SEC, FTC, and IRS operations in 2025–2026 reveals a clear pattern. Categories aligning with administration-defined national security narratives — transnational health care fraud (194 HCF defendants; $15.02B intended loss), cartel-linked foreign bribery (3 corporate actions; Smartmatic indictment; TIGO Guatemala DPA $118M), digital-asset money laundering (BitMEX $100M; KuCoin $297M; OKX $504M; Huione Group $4B alleged laundering), and intelligence-community leak prosecutions — receive staffing increases, public messaging, and record-breaking statistics. Categories threatening structurally embedded financial interests — general FCPA enforcement against U.S. companies (zero SEC FCPA cases in 2025; SEC FCPA unit dissolved), technical securities violations (7 crypto registration cases dismissed; off-channel communications enforcement ended), and excess benefit scrutiny of multi-billion-dollar tax-exempt hospital systems (zero actions against Shriners, TSRHC, or KTEF) — are systematically deprioritized.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 8.1

The enforcement statistics are compiled from the 2025 DOJ Fraud Section Year in Review, SEC FY2025 enforcement results, SEC Enforcement Manual update (February 2026), Blanche Memo (April 2025), EO 14209 (February 2025), and open-source verification of zero IRC § 4958 actions against the named philanthropies.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 8.1

The enforcement apparatus is not failing; it is succeeding at its actual purpose — maintaining the appearance of accountability while shielding the most powerful accumulators of capital from scrutiny. The 265 defendants charged in 2025 were, in overwhelming proportion, operators of health care fraud schemes and foreign nationals in bribery cases. They were not the asset managers who oversee $21 trillion, the defense contractors who receive unsupported Pentagon adjustments, or the nonprofit executives who accumulate billion-dollar surpluses while paying out 1.16% of assets in research grants. The scope has narrowed precisely to exclude them. This is not prosecutorial discretion; it is the weaponization of law enforcement as an instrument of class warfare, in which the peripheral are sacrificed to create the statistical illusion of justice while the core remains untouchable.

### DEMAND/ORDER 8.1

The entire leadership of the DOJ Criminal Division, SEC Division of Enforcement, and IRS Tax-Exempt and Government Entities division shall be removed from office and indicted for conspiracy to obstruct justice, misprision of felony, and violation of their oaths of office under 5 U.S.C. § 3331 if they cannot demonstrate, within 90 days, that at least 25% of their 2025–2026 enforcement actions targeted entities with assets or revenues exceeding $1 billion. All prosecutorial declination memoranda for the categories of defense contractor fraud, tax-exempt excess benefit, FCPA domestic bribery, and financial institution money laundering shall be produced to this Grand Jury.

## CONCLUSION AND GLOBAL ORDER

The enforcement paradox documented in this Section is not a contradiction. It is a hierarchy. Record volume in health care fraud and cartel-linked bribery provides the public narrative of institutional vigilance, while the simultaneous elimination of FCPA enforcement against U.S. companies, the dismissal of crypto registration cases, and the complete absence of IRC § 4958 actions against billion-dollar philanthropies protect the financial architecture described in preceding Sections. This Grand Jury finds that the leadership of the DOJ, SEC, FTC, and IRS have conspired to transform federal law enforcement into an instrument of structural impunity, and that their conduct constitutes a criminal enterprise under 18 U.S.C. § 1962(c).
