## PART VII: THE CORRESPONDENT BANKING HIERARCHY — The Global Pyramid

### 7.1 The Three Tiers

**Tier 1: Central Banks** — Issue base money, settle interbank payments, lender of last resort.

**Tier 2: G-SIBs / Primary Dealers** — Hold correspondent accounts at central banks, trade directly with central banks, provide liquidity to Tier 3.

**Tier 3: Regional and National Banks** — Hold correspondent accounts at Tier 2 banks, serve local businesses and consumers, no direct central bank access.

**Tier 4: Local Banks and Payment Processors** — Hold accounts at Tier 3 banks, serve individual customers.

### 7.2 The Correspondent Account as Control Point

When a Nigerian bank wants to process a U.S. dollar payment, it must hold a correspondent account at a U.S. bank (typically a G-SIB). That U.S. bank holds its dollar reserves at the Federal Reserve.

**De-risking:** Since 2008, major U.S. and European banks have closed correspondent accounts for banks in developing countries. The stated reason: AML/CFT compliance costs. The actual effect: **financial exclusion of entire regions** from the dollar system.
