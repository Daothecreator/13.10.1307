## FRAMING — WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS AND WHAT IT PROVES

This dossier takes two propositions and submits them to the only test that matters: the public record.

**Proposition 1.** Law-enforcement agencies do not merely investigate criminal infrastructure — they have repeatedly created it, owned it, operated it, and profited from its growth, sometimes for years, before harvest. The record on this is not inference. It is indictment, judgment, and the agencies' own press releases.

**Proposition 2.** Large corporations do not merely coexist with war, fraud, laundering, and deception — specific, named, listed companies have documented business models whose revenue lines move with conflict intensity, and documented histories of processing the proceeds and tools of crime at industrial scale, resolved overwhelmingly by negotiated settlements in which the corporation admits the facts and no executive goes to prison.

Everything below is DOCUMENTED, with the record class named per row: federal indictment, deferred prosecution agreement (DPA), guilty plea, regulatory assessment, corporate 10-K, official agency press release, or judgment. Where the record supports a broader structural claim, the claim is labeled STRUCTURAL and the mechanism is stated explicitly, with the documented instances enumerated. The through-line is the causal chain: who built the platform, who profited from the platform, who paid when the platform was exposed, and who did not.

## PART I — THE STATE AS PLATFORM OPERATOR

### 1.1 The documented pattern

Between 2015 and 2021, Western law-enforcement agencies ran the three largest criminal communications platforms on earth, or seized and continued them, harvesting their entire user bases' communications. This is not an allegation. It is the subject of unsealed federal indictments, Europol press releases, and appellate rulings.

**The causal chain, stated plainly:** the state did not infiltrate existing platforms in these cases — it *became* the platform. AN0M was conceived, financed, marketed, and staffed by the FBI and AFP, with criminal distributors used as unwitting sales force. EncroChat and Sky ECC were privately built, but their intelligence value was realized only because the state kept them running — in Sky ECC's case operating the seized servers while a live decryption tap harvested an estimated billion messages. Playpen was run by the FBI itself for two weeks while exploiting every visitor. Each operation follows an identical economic logic: **the platform's value to its criminal users is its trustworthiness; that same trustworthiness, captured, makes it the most effective mass-surveillance device ever deployed against organized crime.** The documented cost is the rule-of-law boundary: courts in multiple jurisdictions spent years ruling on whether the techniques were lawful, and the answer remains split by district and country.

### 1.2 What this does and does not establish

DOCUMENTED: the state has operated criminal-market infrastructure at the largest scale on record; it has done so repeatedly, across agencies and continents, using the same seize-or-build-then-harvest playbook; and the harvest produced the largest law-enforcement result sets in history (27M AN0M messages; ~1B Sky ECC messages).

STRUCTURAL (incentive observation, mechanism stated): a law-enforcement agency that builds or seizes a criminal platform acquires a budget-justifying asset — the bigger the user base it cultivates, the larger the eventual takedown, the larger the demonstrated need for the program. The documented record contains no internal cap on this dynamic; the only documented limiters are judicial admissibility rulings and jurisdictional warrant fights (1.1 rows above).

NOT DOCUMENTED (stated once, as the boundary): the record does not show that the FBI or any Western agency *created the underlying criminal demand*, operates criminal enterprises for profit outside forfeiture law, or "attacks its own citizens" as a policy. Where documented rows approach that boundary — e.g., the NIT's deployment to thousands of computers worldwide, including machines in countries with no nexus to the warrant — the document states exactly what the court record states, and no more.

### 1.3 CURRENT STATUS (OCTOBER 2026)

AN0M convictions continue to be litigated in San Diego and Australia; EncroChat-derived prosecutions are final in France after the 2024 Cour de cassation ruling and continue across the EU; Sky ECC dockets remain active in Belgium and the Netherlands. The documented playbook — seize or build the platform, run it, harvest, prosecute — remains the standing model for Western operations against encrypted criminal infrastructure, publicly advocated by the FBI's own leadership since 2021.

## PART II — CORPORATE CRIME AS BUSINESS MODEL: THE SETTLEMENT LEDGER

### 2.1 The mechanism

For a large corporation, criminal exposure is a priced input. The documented resolution mechanism in the United States and Europe is the deferred prosecution agreement or negotiated settlement: the corporation admits the facts, pays a sum calibrated to ability to pay rather than damage caused, accepts a monitor, and no individual executive is charged. ### 2.3 What the ledger shows, stated as mechanism

**First row, last column, read the totals:** the five largest AML/sanctions settlements of the modern era (HSBC $1.921B, BNP $8.97B, Credit Suisse $2.6B, Glencore $1.1B+, Wells $3B) sum to roughly **$17.6B** — and in every case the corporation admitted the facts and continued operating, and in no case was a senior executive imprisoned. The DOJ's own 2013 record states the constraint: prosecution difficulty scales with institution size. STRUCTURAL conclusion, with mechanism: **when the penalty is negotiated against ability-to-pay and the individuals are never charged, compliance failure becomes a priced operating cost — and the price, documented, is a small fraction of the revenue the underlying business generated.** HSBC's $1.921B was under 15% of one year's revenue; BNP's $8.97B was paid over multiple years while the sanctioned-transactions business it settled for had run for four.

**The laundering channel is upstream of enforcement, not beside it.** The Sinaloa and Norte del Valle proceeds moved through the regulated system for years *while* compliance staff flagged them — the FinCEN Files document the pattern at $2T scale. This is the documented substrate of the proposition that "crime runs through legitimate institutions": not that banks are cartels, but that the cartels' money ran, by the banks' own admissions, through the banks.

### 2.4 CURRENT STATUS (OCTOBER 2026)

The DPA regime remains the standing resolution mechanism for large corporate criminal exposure in the US; no senior banking executive from the documented ledger above has been imprisoned. Boeing's DPA was voided and replaced with a 2024 guilty plea after a documented compliance breach — the exception that demonstrates the rule's existence. The FinCEN/BSA reporting channel that exposed the $2T continues to operate unchanged in structure.

### 1.4 AN0M / Operation Trojan Shield — the full documented chronology

**Genesis (2018).** The FBI's San Diego field office, pursuing Phantom Secure's encrypted-device executives (Ramos pled guilty 2018), obtained from a convicted distributor a new-device concept and a cooperating human source with standing in the criminal encrypted-phone market. The documented design goal, stated by FBI leadership after the fact: "Criminal groups using encrypted communications to thwart law enforcement should no longer feel safe in that space. **Cultivation (2019–2021).** Through criminal wholesalers whose ignorance of the operation was itself a prosecutorial asset — the San Diego indictment charged 17 of them with RICO for distributing the platform — AN0M grew on the documented wreckage of its predecessors: when Phantom Secure fell (2018) and when European platforms were shaken, AN0M absorbed their refugee user bases.
