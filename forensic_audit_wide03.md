## Facet: Iljin Global Automotive Supply Chain

### Key Findings
- **Iljin Global Co., Ltd.** is not independently publicly traded; it is a privately held operating subsidiary of the broader Iljin Group. The group’s listed holding vehicle is **ILJIN Holdings Co., Ltd. (KRX: 015860)**. Other listed affiliates include Iljin Electric (KRX: 103590), Iljin Diamond (KRX: 081000), and Iljin Hysolus (KRX: 271940), but none of these are the wheel-bearing entity under audit. [^stockanalysis][^marklines1]
- Iljin Global reported **consolidated sales of 1,769,957 million KRW (~USD 1.3 bn)** for FY 2024 and a registered capital of only **790 million KRW** (≈USD 580k), indicating heavy leverage of group-level capital and thin equity at the operating entity. [^marklines1]
- Per EMIS data, in 2023 Iljin Global’s net sales grew **1.16%**, but operating profit fell **14.8%** and total assets contracted **4.85%**, suggesting margin compression and balance-sheet shrinkage. [^emis]
- Iljin Global supplies wheel bearings directly to **Hyundai, Kia, GM, Ford, FCA/Stellantis, Mercedes-Benz, BMW, and Maserati**, and acts as a tier-2 supplier to **SKF, NTN, Timken, Schaeffler, and Federal-Mogul** for aftermarket and OEM hub units. [^iljincom][^tradekorea]
- A **2013 NHTSA safety recall (Campaign #13E-055)** involved Iljin Global front wheel bearings sold to Federal-Mogul under the MOOG brand for Ford Heavy Duty Trucks (2005–2010). The wheel speed sensor wires were reversed, disabling ABS and increasing crash risk. [^nhtsa1][^nhtsa2]
- **Labor disputes in India**: Iljin Global India Pvt. Ltd. is a petitioner in ongoing Bombay High Court writ petitions (WP 864/2025, WP 16906/2024) concerning the **Satara Mathadi & Unprotected Labour Board**, indicating classification or labor-rights litigation in a key offshore manufacturing hub. [^indiankanoon1][^indiankanoon2]
- The group maintains an extensive **China footprint** (Beijing ILJIN, Tianjin/Jiangsu ILJIN, Chongqing ILJIN) serving Shanghai GM, Chery, and Beijing Benz, exposing it to U.S. tariff escalation, forced-labor scrutiny, and geopolitical decoupling risk. [^koreapartsinfo][^marklines2]
- Iljin Global continues active **U.S. patent prosecution** (e.g., USPTO application 20250262891 for sensor-integrated wheel bearings) and holds U.S. subsidiary patents via ILJIN USA Corporation, suggesting R&D investment but also IP concentration in jurisdictions subject to ITC actions. [^justia1][^justia3]
- No direct analyst coverage of Iljin Global was found; equity research and market data tracked by the parent holding company or other listed affiliates, leaving the operating entity with limited transparency for forensic review. [^stockanalysis]

### Major Players & Sources
- **ILJIN Holdings Co., Ltd. (KRX: 015860)**: Group holding company with 2.28T KRW revenue (FY 2025); primary source of consolidated financial visibility. [^stockanalysis]
- **ILJIN Bearing Co., Ltd.**: Sister company (wheel bearings, sales 328.3B KRW FY 2024). Often confused with Iljin Global in market reports. [^marklines1]
- **Iljin Global India Pvt. Ltd.**: Offshore production subsidiary; subject to Indian labor court proceedings. [^indiankanoon1]
- **ILJIN USA Corporation / ILJIN Alabama / ILJIN America (Greer, SC)**: North American manufacturing and R&D footprint (Michigan Tech Center). [^marklines1][^koreapartsinfo]
- **ILJIN GmbH (Germany)**: European R&D and sales office. [^koreapartsinfo]
- **Beijing / Tianjin / Jiangsu / Chongqing ILJIN**: China manufacturing cluster serving domestic OEMs and export markets. [^marklines2][^koreapartsinfo]
- **Federal-Mogul (now Tenneco)**: Aftermarket partner involved in the 2013 NHTSA recall. [^nhtsa1]
- **Competitors**: SKF, Schaeffler, NSK, NTN, JTEKT, Timken, GMB, C&U, ZWZ. [^mordor][^factmr]

### Trends & Signals
- **EV transition & lightweighting**: Iljin is developing next-generation lightweight wheel bearings and suspension arms (plastic-composite EV arms) to extend range; this requires capex but could shift mix away from legacy ICE platforms. [^marklines1]
- **Supply chain geopolitical bifurcation**: Rising U.S.–China trade barriers and automotive tariffs are pressuring Korean suppliers with Chinese production to adopt “China-plus-one” strategies or absorb higher logistics costs. [^eiu][^cepr]
- **Market consolidation**: Global automotive bearing market is concentrated among SKF (~20%), Schaeffler (~18%), NSK (~12%), Timken (~10%), and NTN (~8%). Iljin is categorized as a mid-tier, cost-efficient Asian player without disclosed market share. [^verifiedmarket][^factmr]
- **ESG and carbon neutrality**: Iljin Slovakia’s CSR report targets carbon neutrality for Scope 1+2 by 2030, Scope 3 by 2040, and net zero by 2050; supplier audits for labor and ethics are being implemented. [^iljinslovakia]

### Controversies & Conflicting Claims
- **Recall vs. quality awards**: Iljin publicly markets “GM Supplier of the Year” (8 consecutive years) and Ford World Excellence Awards, yet the 2013 NHTSA recall demonstrates a manufacturing process control failure that disabled safety-critical ABS systems. [^iljinslovakia][^nhtsa1]
- **Capitalization mismatch**: Iljin Global’s registered capital is only 790 million KRW against 1.77 trillion KRW in consolidated sales—a 1:2,240 equity-to-sales ratio. This suggests extreme thin-capitalization, extensive intercompany financing, or off-balance-sheet group support, which warrants forensic scrutiny of transfer pricing and debt guarantees. [^marklines1]
- **Labor board litigation in India**: The Bombay High Court writ petitions indicate a regulatory or union dispute over labor board classification; the exact substance is not fully public, but the docket shows active litigation through January 2026. [^indiankanoon1][^indiankanoon2]
- **Iljin Global vs. Iljin Group confusion**: Multiple market reports list “Iljin Group” or “Iljin Co., Ltd.” as a bearing competitor, often conflating the four separate entities (Bearing, Global, Co., Ltd., and Bearing Art). This opacity complicates competitive benchmarking and credit analysis. [^mordor][^factmr]

### Recommended Deep-Dive Areas
- **Intercompany financing & thin capitalization**: Iljin Global’s 790M KRW capital against 1.77T KRW sales demands a detailed review of parent loans, guarantees, and cash-pooling arrangements to assess solvency risk and profit-shifting. [^marklines1]
- **China exposure & forced-labor compliance**: Deep-dive into Beijing/Tianjin/Jiangsu/Chongqing operations, including Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA) exposure, supplier audits, and contingency plans for U.S. tariff escalation. [^marklines2][^eiu]
- **India labor litigation**: Obtain the full Bombay High Court writ petition records and local labor board orders to assess contingent liability, back-wage exposure, and operational disruption risk. [^indiankanoon1][^indiankanoon2]
- **NHTSA recall root cause & ongoing quality metrics**: The 2013 ABS sensor recall should be cross-checked with later NHTSA complaints, OEM quality scorecards, and warranty reserve adequacy. [^nhtsa1][^nhtsa2]
- **Patent landscape & IP risk**: Map Iljin Global’s sensor-integrated bearing patents against SKF, NTN, and Schaeffler portfolios to assess infringement exposure and licensing potential. [^justia1][^justia3]
- **Subsidiary network cash flows**: Iljin India, Iljin Slovakia, Iljin USA, and Iljin China are not separately audited in public filings. A consolidated cash-flow forensic map is needed to identify trapped cash, tax-haven routing, and royalty flows. [^marklines1][^iljinslovakia]
