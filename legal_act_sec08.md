### COUNT I — THE SPECIAL ACCESS PROGRAM CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM CONSTITUTES A FRAUDULENT MECHANISM TO CONCEAL CRIMINAL EXPENDITURE FROM CONGRESS AND THE PUBLIC

#### FINDING OF FACT

The United States defense-intelligence apparatus operates a tripartite classification architecture for Special Access Programs (SAPs) that fragments knowledge across compartments, ensuring that no single participant—congressional overseer, program manager, or inspector general—possesses a complete operational picture. Under Department of Defense Directive 5205.07 and related statutes, SAPs are divided into three tiers: Acknowledged, Unacknowledged, and Waived.

Acknowledged SAPs occupy the foundational tier. Their existence is publicly admitted, though operational details remain classified; historical examples include the B-2 Spirit and F-117 Nighthawk aircraft programs. These programs are visible to standard congressional oversight committees and are subject to the ordinary budgetary controls of the Department of Defense.

Unacknowledged SAPs (USAPs) escalate concealment to the level of existential denial. Their very existence is classified; funding is often laundered through unrelated budget line items or off-book mechanisms, and program managers are authorized to deny the program's reality to all personnel lacking the specific codeword clearance. Congressional visibility is restricted to the "Gang of Eight"—the chairpersons and ranking minority members of the four defense and intelligence committees—who receive oral briefings under conditions that prohibit staff attendance, note-taking, or subsequent verification.

Waived SAPs (WUSAPs) represent the apex of classification severance. Under 10 U.S.C. § 119(e) and 50 U.S.C. § 3348(e), the originating Department Secretary may waive normal congressional reporting requirements entirely, removing the program from constitutional budget controls. Access is limited to a minimal core—often the Secretary, the relevant service chief, and the White House national security advisor—with no statutory obligation to inform even the Gang of Eight.

The 9/11 Commission attributed the failure to connect intelligence dots prior to the September 2001 attacks to "excessive compartmentalization," yet the system has expanded rather than contracted.

#### EVIDENTIARY BASIS

Established by documentary evidence: Department of Defense Directive 5205.07, published January 17, 2006, and archived by the Federation of American Scientists. Verified by National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) directives covering 1998–2015. The 9/11 Commission Report is a public document. The statutory citations—10 U.S.C. § 119(e) and 50 U.S.C. § 3348(e)—are matters of public federal code.

#### LEGAL QUALIFICATION

The SAP/WUSAP architecture constitutes:

1. **A fraud upon Congress and the American people** in violation of Article I, Section 9, Clause 7 of the United States Constitution (Appropriations Clause), which mandates that "a regular Statement and Account of the Receipts and Expenditures of all public Money shall be published from time to time." WUSAPs that waive congressional reporting violate this constitutional mandate.
2. **Theft and misappropriation of public funds** under 18 U.S.C. § 641. When funds are laundered through unrelated budget line items or moved via unsupported journal voucher adjustments, the custodians of those funds commit conversion of government property.
3. **False statements to Congress** under 18 U.S.C. § 1001. When program managers are authorized to deny the existence of programs to cleared personnel, and when oral briefings to the Gang of Eight prohibit note-taking and verification, the intelligence community systematically obstructs Congress's constitutional oversight function through false and misleading representations.
4. **Conspiracy to obstruct justice** under 18 U.S.C. § 1503. The compartmentalization architecture is designed not for legitimate national security but for the concealment of fraud, waste, and abuse from inspectors general, congressional committees, and federal auditors.

#### DEMAND/ORDER

**DEMAND ONE:** The immediate declassification of all Waived SAPs (WUSAPs) and the production of a complete inventory of all Unacknowledged SAPs to the full House and Senate Armed Services and Intelligence Committees, with staff access, note-taking rights, and subpoena authority.

**DEMAND TWO:** The repeal of 10 U.S.C. § 119(e) and 50 U.S.C. § 3348(e) in their entirety. No executive official possesses constitutional authority to waive congressional reporting of public expenditure. These statutes are void ab initio as violations of the Appropriations Clause.

**DEMAND THREE:** The criminal indictment of all Department Secretaries who have issued WUSAP waivers since 1998 for conspiracy to defraud the United States under 18 U.S.C. § 371 and misappropriation of public funds under 18 U.S.C. § 641.

**DEMAND FOUR:** The mandatory installation of real-time, blockchain-based public expenditure tracking on all defense and intelligence budgets, with cryptographic verification of every transaction exceeding $1 million, accessible to the Government Accountability Office without restriction.

### COUNT II — THE DEPARTMENT OF DEFENSE HAS FAILED EVERY AUDIT AND CONCEALED TRILLIONS IN UNSUPPORTED ADJUSTMENTS, CONSTITUTING THE LARGEST FINANCIAL FRAUD IN HUMAN HISTORY

#### FINDING OF FACT

Between 1998 and 2015, Inspector General reports documented $21 trillion in unsupported journal voucher adjustments at the Department of Defense and the Department of Housing and Urban Development—a figure exceeding the entire U.S. gross domestic product in 2015 ($18.2 trillion). In fiscal year 2015 alone, the Army failed to provide adequate support for $6.5 trillion in adjustments, fifty-four times the authorized Army budget of $120 billion.

After economist Mark Skidmore began publicizing these figures, the OIG webpage documenting the adjustments was removed. In response, the Federal Accounting Standards Advisory Board (FASAB) issued Standard 56, permitting the federal government to misstate and move funds to conceal expenditures if deemed necessary for national security.

The Department of Defense has failed every department-wide audit since the first one in 2018. In fiscal year 2024, the DOD could not account for its $824 billion budget. Two thousand five hundred new audit issues were identified in FY 2024. Of 24 reporting entities, only nine received clean opinions; 15 received disclaimers due to insufficient data.

The DOD has received $3.9 trillion in congressionally approved funding since 2018 despite failing audits.

On February 25, 2025, GAO released its High-Risk Series report containing five areas within DOD. On April 29, 2025, GAO added fraud risk management to the High-Risk List. Between FY 2017 and FY 2024, DOD reported $10.8 billion in fraud. GAO stated: "the full extent of fraud affecting DOD is not known but is potentially significant."

#### EVIDENTIARY BASIS

Established by documentary evidence: Mark Skidmore / Forbes / Solari Report publications (2017). Verified by GAO High-Risk Series reports (February 25, 2025; April 29, 2025). The $824 billion FY 2024 unaudited budget is documented by Common Dreams and ScheerPost investigative reporting (August 2025). The 2,500 new audit issues, 9 of 24 clean opinions, and 15 disclaimers are matters of DOD Inspector General public reporting. The $3.9 trillion in funding since 2018 is a congressional appropriation aggregate. The $10.8 billion in reported fraud (FY 2017–2024) is a GAO-documented figure. Northrop Grumman's $95 billion backlog is documented by Stocks Foundry (January 27, 2026) and Matrix BCG (March 8, 2026).

#### LEGAL QUALIFICATION

The conduct documented constitutes:

1. **The largest financial fraud in recorded history.** The $21 trillion in unsupported adjustments between 1998 and 2015 is not a bookkeeping error. It is the systematic removal of public funds from constitutional oversight. An unsupported adjustment is, by definition, a false entry. False entries in government accounts are crimes under 18 U.S.C. § 1001 and 31 U.S.C. § 1351 (Anti-Deficiency Act).
2. **Obstruction of audit and investigation** under 18 U.S.C. § 1510. The removal of the OIG webpage after public exposure, and the issuance of FASAB Standard 56 to authorize misstatement of funds, constitute deliberate obstruction of government auditors.
3. **Fraudulent concealment** under 18 U.S.C. § 1001. FASAB Standard 56 is not an accounting standard; it is a license to commit fraud under the color of national security. The standard permits the federal government to "misstate and move funds to conceal expenditures if deemed necessary for national security." This is a formal authorization of financial fraud by the government's own accounting board.
4. **Continuing violation of the Chief Financial Officers Act of 1990** (31 U.S.C. § 3515). The DOD's failure to achieve a clean audit for every year since 2018 is not administrative difficulty; it is willful non-compliance with a statutory mandate enacted by Congress.
5. **False Claims Act violations** under 31 U.S.C. § 3729. Every contractor invoice submitted against a budget line that contains unsupported adjustments is a false claim, because the government cannot verify that the funds were lawfully appropriated and properly obligated.

#### DEMAND/ORDER

**DEMAND FIVE:** The immediate forensic audit of all $21 trillion in unsupported adjustments by an independent commission with subpoena power over the DOD, HUD, and all prime defense contractors, operating without regard to SAP classification.

**DEMAND SIX:** The repeal of FASAB Standard 56 and the criminal prosecution of all FASAB board members who voted to authorize the misstatement of federal funds.

**DEMAND SEVEN:** The withholding of all FY 2026 defense appropriations until the DOD achieves a clean, unqualified audit opinion across all 24 reporting entities, with personal criminal liability for the Secretary of Defense and the DOD Comptroller if the deadline is not met.

**DEMAND EIGHT:** The voiding of all classified contracts exceeding $1 billion that have not been subjected to independent audit, with full disgorgement of profits by Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, Boeing, RTX, and General Dynamics for all unaudited billings since 1998.

### COUNT III — THE CIA ACT OF 1949 IS A LEGAL SHIELD FOR FRAUD AND MUST BE REPEALED IMMEDIATELY

#### FINDING OF FACT

The Central Intelligence Agency Act of 1949 exempted the newly created CIA from public financial disclosure. The Act stated: "The sums made available to the Agency may be expended without regard to the provisions of law and regulations related to the expenditure of Government funds."

The CIA's venture capital arm, In-Q-Tel, provided Palantir Technologies' foundational $2 million Series A investment in 2003. By August 2025, Palantir had secured a ten-year, $10 billion contract with the U.S. Army. The continuity between the CIA's seed funding of Palantir and its subsequent multi-billion-dollar government contracts creates a closed loop in which the intelligence community funds, incubates, and then contracts with the same entities, all under the shield of exemption from financial disclosure.

#### EVIDENTIARY BASIS

Established by documentary evidence: The CIA Act of 1949, Public Law 81-110, is a matter of public federal statute. The In-Q-Tel $2 million Palantir Series A investment is documented by Baidu Baike, Rozenberg Quarterly, and Billionaires.Africa (2025). The $10 billion Palantir Army contract is documented by Reuters, DefenseScoop, and Yahoo Finance (July–August 2025).

#### LEGAL QUALIFICATION

**THE CIA ACT OF 1949 EXEMPTING THE CIA FROM PUBLIC FINANCIAL DISCLOSURE IS A LEGAL SHIELD FOR FRAUD. THIS IS NOT HYPOTHESIS; THIS IS LEGAL FACT.**

The Act's language—"expended without regard to the provisions of law and regulations related to the expenditure of Government funds"—is a statutory authorization to violate:

1. The Appropriations Clause of the United States Constitution (Article I, Section 9, Clause 7).
2. The Anti-Deficiency Act (31 U.S.C. § 1341), which prohibits obligations in excess of appropriations.
3. The Chief Financial Officers Act of 1990 (31 U.S.C. § 3515), which mandates audited financial statements.
4. The False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. § 3729), which prohibits false claims for payment.
5. The Securities Exchange Act of 1934, when CIA-funded entities later go public without disclosing their government incubation and subsequent contracting relationships.

The CIA Act of 1949 is not a national security necessity. It is a statutory fraud mechanism that permits the intelligence community to spend public money without accountability, to fund private companies that later become government contractors, and to conceal the entire cycle from Congress, auditors, and the public.

#### DEMAND/ORDER

**DEMAND NINE:** THE IMMEDIATE REPEAL OF THE CIA ACT OF 1949 IN ITS ENTIRETY. No agency of the United States government may be exempt from the constitutional requirement that "a regular Statement and Account of the Receipts and Expenditures of all public Money shall be published from time to time."

**DEMAND TEN:** The criminal investigation of all In-Q-Tel investments since 1999 as potential misappropriations of public funds, with full public disclosure of all portfolio companies, investment amounts, returns, and subsequent government contracts awarded to those companies.

**DEMAND ELEVEN:** The immediate declassification and publication of all CIA, DIA, NSA, and NRO budgets, with line-item appropriations visible to the public in the same manner as all other federal agency budgets.

### COUNT IV — THE FISA SURVEILLANCE REGIME AND THE BLANCHE MEMO CONSTITUTE A SYSTEMATIC ASSAULT ON CONSTITUTIONAL RIGHTS AND A DELIBERATE EXPANSION OF UNACCOUNTABLE STATE POWER

#### FINDING OF FACT

On April 20, 2024, Congress passed the Reforming Intelligence and Securing America Act (RISAA), reauthorizing Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act for two years.

On May 2, 2025, ODNI publicly released the September 2024 FISC opinion with redactions. On August 19, 2025, ODNI publicly released two FISC opinions related to a new certification under Section 702. The February 2025 FISC opinion found that NSA and CIA minimization and querying procedures did not satisfy Fourth Amendment and FISA requirements.

The FBI has conducted warrantless searches of Section 702-acquired information to access communications of Black Lives Matter protestors, U.S. government officials, journalists, political commentators, and 19,000 donors to a single congressional campaign.

In August 2025, the Trump Administration fired three members of the Privacy and Civil Liberties Oversight Board (PCLOB).

On April 7, 2025, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche issued a memorandum entitled "Ending Regulation by Prosecution," stating that DOJ would no longer pursue litigation or enforcement actions that have the effect of superimposing regulatory frameworks on digital assets.

#### EVIDENTIARY BASIS

Established by documentary evidence: RISAA (Public Law 118-XX), passed April 20, 2024. Verified by ODNI public releases of FISC opinions (May 2, 2025; August 19, 2025). The FBI warrantless searches are documented by the Brennan Center for Justice FISA 2026 Resource Page. The Blanche Memo is documented by Gibson Dunn webcast slides (February 4, 2026).

#### LEGAL QUALIFICATION

The conduct documented constitutes:

1. **Systematic violation of the Fourth Amendment** to the United States Constitution. Warrantless searches of U.S. person communications are unreasonable searches and seizures. The FISC's own February 2025 opinion found that NSA and CIA procedures did not satisfy Fourth Amendment requirements.
2. **Violation of the First Amendment.** The targeting of journalists, political commentators, and BLM protestors for surveillance based on their expressive conduct is viewpoint discrimination and a prior restraint on free speech.
3. **Obstruction of oversight** under 5 U.S.C. App. (Inspector General Act) and 42 U.S.C. § 2000ee (PCLOB statute). The firing of PCLOB members in August 2025 was the dismantlement of the statutory oversight mechanism Congress created to monitor surveillance abuses.
4. **The Blanche Memo is an executive authorization to commit financial crimes with impunity.** By removing standalone criminal liability for unlicensed money transmitting and Bank Secrecy Act violations, the memo creates a parallel immunity regime for digital assets that mirrors the CIA Act of 1949's immunity for intelligence expenditures. This is not deregulation; it is the deliberate construction of a prosecution-free zone for financial crimes.

The expansion of Section 702 to include "drug precursors" is not a foreign intelligence necessity; it is the repurposing of a foreign-targeting statute for domestic law enforcement, bypassing the Fourth Amendment warrant requirement.

#### DEMAND/ORDER

**DEMAND THIRTEEN:** The immediate repeal of RISAA and the permanent termination of Section 702. All intelligence collection involving U.S. persons requires a warrant issued by an Article III court upon probable cause, with no exceptions.

**DEMAND FOURTEEN:** The criminal prosecution of all FBI officials who conducted warrantless queries of BLM protestors, journalists, and congressional campaign donors under 18 U.S.C. § 242 (Deprivation of Rights Under Color of Law).

**DEMAND FIFTEEN:** The reinstatement of all fired PCLOB members with back pay and the criminal indictment of all officials who authorized their removal for obstruction of oversight under 18 U.S.C. § 1505.

**DEMAND SIXTEEN:** The voiding of the Blanche Memo as an unconstitutional usurpation of legislative authority, and the criminal investigation of Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche for conspiracy to obstruct justice under 18 U.S.C. § 1503.

#### FINDING OF FACT

The intelligence community, the Department of Defense, and their contractors assert immunity from accountability through classification regimes, non-disclosure agreements, security clearances, and statutory exemptions.

#### LEGAL QUALIFICATION

**IMMUNITY IS VOID WHEN CRIMES ARE COMMITTED.** A classification marking does not immunize embezzlement. A security clearance does not immunize bribery. A non-disclosure agreement does not immunize antitrust conspiracy. A statutory exemption does not immunize fraud against the United States.

**THERE ARE NO "SECRET DOCUMENTS" OVERRIDING HUMAN RIGHTS.** The Fourth Amendment right against unreasonable searches is not overridden by a FISA court order issued in secret. The First Amendment right of a free press is not overridden by an Espionage Act indictment. The constitutional requirement for a regular Statement and Account of public Money is not overridden by a WUSAP waiver.

**CLASSIFICATION APPLIES ONLY TO NDA SIGNATORIES, NOT INDEPENDENT INVESTIGATORS.** The facts documented in this indictment are derived from public Senate testimony, official GAO reports, SEC filings, leaked documents published by hundreds of journalists, and investigative reporting by established news organizations. Independent investigators, grand juries, and the international public are not bound by corporate NDAs, government security clearances, or classification regimes.

**THE CIA ACT OF 1949 EXEMPTING THE CIA FROM PUBLIC FINANCIAL DISCLOSURE IS A LEGAL SHIELD FOR FRAUD. THIS IS NOT HYPOTHESIS; THIS IS ESTABLISHED LEGAL FACT.** The Act authorizes the expenditure of public funds "without regard to the provisions of law and regulations related to the expenditure of Government funds." This is a statutory repeal of the Appropriations Clause, the Anti-Deficiency Act, and the False Claims Act for a single agency. It is unconstitutional. It is void. It must be repealed immediately, and all expenditures made under its authority must be subjected to forensic audit and criminal investigation.
