### 1.3 FCPA 2025 — Trump Administration Enforcement Pause & Dismissals

**Entity/Case Name:** Executive Order 14209 — FCPA Enforcement Pause / United States v. Coburn & Schwartz (Cognizant)

**Established Facts:**
- On February 10, 2025, President Trump issued Executive Order 14209, "Pausing Foreign Corrupt Practices Act Enforcement to Further American Economic and National Security."
- The Executive Order directed the Attorney General to cease initiation of any new FCPA investigation or enforcement action for 180 days (with option to extend another 180 days), and to review all ongoing investigations or enforcement actions.
- On April 2, 2025, DOJ moved to dismiss with prejudice the long-running FCPA indictment against former Cognizant executives Gordon Coburn and Steven Schwartz, citing "the recent assessment of the Executive Order's application to this matter."
- The court dismissed the charges on April 3, 2025.
- The case had been pending since February 2019 — over six years.
- Coburn was Cognizant's former President; Schwartz was Cognizant's former Chief Legal Officer.
- They had been indicted for allegedly authorizing a $2 million payment for the benefit of an Indian government official to expedite permits for a corporate campus in Tamil Nadu.
- On July 15, 2025, the SEC filed a joint stipulation to dismiss its parallel civil FCPA case against Coburn and Schwartz with prejudice.
- DOJ also terminated early the compliance monitorship of Glencore plc (15 months ahead of schedule) in March 2025.
- The DOJ discontinued approximately half of its open FCPA investigations following the Executive Order review.
- Early termination of at least four deferred prosecution agreements (DPAs), a non-prosecution agreement (NPA), and a corporate monitorship occurred in 2025.
- Source: https://www.gibsondunn.com/2025-year-end-fcpa-update/

**Named Individuals:**
- Gordon Coburn — former President, Cognizant Technology Solutions
- Steven Schwartz — former Chief Legal Officer, Cognizant Technology Solutions

## 2. SEC ENFORCEMENT DIVISION — 2024-2025 MAJOR ACTIONS

### 2.1 SEC Enforcement 2025 — Transition and Slowdown

**Entity/Case Name:** SEC Enforcement Division — FY 2025 Overview

**Established Facts:**
- On January 20, 2025, SEC Chair Gary Gensler resigned.
- Commissioner Mark Uyeda served as Acting Chair until Paul Atkins was sworn in as new SEC Chair on April 21, 2025.
- In FY 2025 (ended September 30, 2025), new enforcement actions fell to 313 — the lowest in a decade and down 27% from FY 2024.
- Total monetary settlements declined 45% to $808 million — the lowest since FY 2012.
- Record-low disgorgement and prejudgment interest total of $108 million.
- Only four actions against public companies and/or subsidiaries were initiated under Acting Chair Uyeda and Chair Atkins.
- The SEC workforce declined by approximately 15% from the previous administration.
- The SEC's FCPA Unit was quietly disbanded in 2025, including the departure of its chief and deputy chief.
- The SEC did not bring any new FCPA enforcement actions in FY 2025.
- Source: https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/sec-enforcement-2025-year-in-review

**Named Individuals:**
- Gary Gensler — former SEC Chair (resigned January 20, 2025)
- Paul Atkins — SEC Chair (sworn in April 21, 2025)
- Mark Uyeda — Acting SEC Chair (January 21 – April 20, 2025)
- Margaret Ryan — Enforcement Director

**Dollar Amounts & Dates:**
- FY 2025 total monetary settlements: $808 million (down 45% from FY 2024)
- FY 2025 disgorgement and prejudgment interest: $108 million (record low)
- 93% of actions against public companies in FY 2025 were initiated under former Chair Gensler

**Legal Violations Established or Suspected:**

### 2.2 SEC Insider Trading Enforcement 2024-2025

**Entity/Case Name:** SEC v. Panuwat / "Shadow Trading" / Multiple Insider Trading Actions

**Established Facts:**
- Insider trading remained an enforcement priority under both Gensler and Atkins SEC administrations.
- In 2024, the SEC achieved a significant litigation victory in SEC v. Panuwat, establishing "shadow trading" liability — using material nonpublic information about one company to trade securities of another company.
- On March 4, 2025, the SEC charged German national Eamma Safi and Singaporean national Zhi Ge with Rule 10b-5 violations for alleged involvement in an international insider trading ring that generated more than $17.5 million in illegal profits.
- In August 2025, the SEC charged a former director of a biopharmaceutical company, along with two family members and two friends, with insider trading ahead of an acquisition announcement, resulting in more than $500,000 in illicit profits.
- On September 5, 2025, the SEC filed and settled insider trading charges against Ryan Squillante, former head of equity trading at Denver-based Irving Investors, who allegedly used confidential information to trade securities of at least 10 public companies, netting nearly $217,000 in trading profits.
- In January 2025, the SEC settled charges against an electrical engineering professor for insider trading in relation to the acquisition of a company in the radio frequency filters industry, resulting in $360,673 in illegal trading profits. The professor agreed to a five-year officer and director bar and to pay over $780,000 in civil penalties and disgorgement.

## 3. FTC — 2024-2025 ANTITRUST ACTIONS

### 3.1 FTC/DOJ Antitrust 2025 — Shift in Enforcement Philosophy

**Entity/Case Name:** Federal Trade Commission / DOJ Antitrust Division — 2025 Policy Shift

**Established Facts:**
- Under the second Trump administration, the FTC and DOJ signaled a significant departure from Biden administration antitrust policies.
- The agencies retained the 2023 Merger Guidelines to preserve stability and "agency credibility."
- However, they adopted a more pragmatic approach to merger review.
- Greater willingness to accept structural remedies (divestitures) in lieu of litigating merger challenges.
- Return to more traditional theories of harm focused on consumer welfare.
- Less emphasis on novel theories such as labor market impacts and bundling/conglomerate theories.
- In May 2025, the FTC cleared Synopsys, Inc.'s $35 billion acquisition of Ansys, Inc. subject to divestiture of certain assets.
- The DOJ settled its challenge to Hewlett Packard Enterprise's deal with Juniper Networks after HPE agreed to divest a business and license technology to rivals.
- In November 2025, the DOJ settled claims against RealPage, Inc., whose revenue-management software was allegedly used to inflate rents; settlement prohibits use of competitors' nonpublic information in setting rental prices.
- Source: https://www.foley.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Recent-Developments-for-Private-Company-MA-2026_final.pdf

**Named Individuals:**
- Lina Khan — former FTC Chair (departed January 2025)

**Dollar Amounts & Dates:**
- Synopsys/Ansys: $35 billion acquisition (cleared May 2025 with divestitures)
- HPE/Juniper Networks: settled with divestitures (2025)
- RealPage, Inc.: settlement November 2025

### 3.2 FTC v. Meta — Instagram/WhatsApp Acquisitions

**Entity/Case Name:** Federal Trade Commission v. Meta Platforms, Inc.

**Established Facts:**
- In November 2024, Judge James Boasberg of the D.C. District Court denied Meta's motion for summary judgment.
- The court ruled that substantial evidence supports allegations that Meta's Instagram and WhatsApp acquisitions violated antitrust laws by maintaining a monopoly in personal social networking.
- Internal documents and Mark Zuckerberg's statements demonstrated these platforms were competitors when acquired.
- The court found the FTC sufficiently argued that the acquisitions harmed competition by reducing innovation, limiting consumer choice, and weakening privacy protections.
- The case was set for trial in April 2025.
- In 2025, the court ruled for Meta on the merits, finding the agency failed to prove monopoly power once the market was defined to include TikTok and YouTube.
- Source: https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/antitrust-and-competition-technology-9930010/

**Named Individuals:**
- Mark Zuckerberg — CEO, Meta Platforms, Inc.
- Judge James Boasberg — D.D.C.

**Dollar Amounts & Dates:**
- Meta acquisitions: Instagram ($1 billion, 2012); WhatsApp ($19 billion, 2014)
- Summary judgment denied: November 2024
- Trial: April 2025
- Ruling for Meta: 2025

### 4.2 False Claims Act 2025 — Continued Enforcement

**Entity/Case Name:** DOJ False Claims Act Enforcement — FY 2025 (through July)

**Established Facts:**
- From January 2025 through July 31, 2025, 171 publicly announced settlements and resolutions occurred.

### 4.4 Whistleblower Retaliation — Supreme Court 2024

**Entity/Case Name:** Murray v. UBS Securities, LLC (U.S. Supreme Court, 2024)

**Established Facts:**
- Trevor Murray, a research strategist at UBS, was terminated after reporting that supervisors pressured him to skew research to support their business positions — conduct that would have violated securities regulations.
- The Supreme Court unanimously held that under Sarbanes-Oxley's whistleblower protection provisions, an employee needs to demonstrate only that the protected activity was a contributing factor to the adverse employment action — not that the employer had retaliatory intent.
- Once the employee demonstrates this, the burden shifts to the employer to prove it would have taken the same action regardless of the protected disclosure.
- This decision significantly lowered the evidentiary bar for successful whistleblower retaliation claims.
- Source: https://voxwel.com/blogs/employment-law-cases-2025

**Named Individuals:**
- Trevor Murray — plaintiff/whistleblower, former UBS research strategist

**Dollar Amounts & Dates:**
- Supreme Court decision: 2024

- Sarbanes-Oxley Act, Section 806 (18 U.S.C. § 1514A) — whistleblower retaliation
