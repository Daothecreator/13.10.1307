## 2. THE VANGUARD GROUP, INC.

### Established Facts
- **Ownership Structure**: Vanguard is not owned by public shareholders. It is owned by the mutual funds it manages, which are in turn owned by fund shareholders. This structure eliminates outside ownership but concentrates voting power in the hands of Vanguard's internal governance apparatus.
  - Source: Vanguard; InvestGuiding — https://investguiding.com/article/lockheed-martin-stock-ownership-who-owns-lockheed-martin-in-2024
- **Proxy Voting Coordination with BlackRock**: Vanguard has entered into a **letter agreement with BlackRock** pursuant to which, for any annual or special meeting of BlackRock's shareholders or solicitation of consents with respect to the election of directors, **Vanguard will grant a proxy to vote any shares of BlackRock common stock owned by Vanguard in excess of 10% of the outstanding BlackRock shares in proportion to the votes of other shares of BlackRock common stock** on matters presented for shareholder approval.
  - Source: BlackRock 2025 Proxy Statement, Schedule 13G/A disclosure — https://s24.q4cdn.com/856567660/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/PRO013216_91_Blackrock_Proxy-Statement-2025.pdf
- **Vanguard Holdings in BlackRock**: Per Amendment No. 8 to Schedule 13G filed February 13, 2024: 0 shares sole voting power; 180,193 shares shared voting power; 12,258,524 shares sole dispositive power; 631,484 shares shared dispositive power.
  - Source: BlackRock 2025 Proxy Statement — https://s24.q4cdn.com/856567660/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/PRO013216_91_Blackrock_Proxy-Statement-2025.pdf
- **Mirror Voting Practices**: Vanguard has entered into agreements with various state, federal, and non-U.S. regulators and with certain issuers that limit the amount of shares that the funds may vote at their discretion. For these securities, any additional shares generally are **voted in the same proportion as votes cast by the issuer's entire shareholder base (i.e., mirror voted)**. The Board has adopted policies that will result in certain funds mirror voting a higher proportion of shares they own in a regulated issuer.
  - Source: Vanguard Market Neutral Fund Prospectus (SEC filing) — http://pdf.secdatabase.com/1903/0001683863-25-004072.pdf
- **Index Fund Control Over Major Corporations**: Vanguard appears as a top-3 shareholder in virtually every major U.S. defense contractor and S&P 500 company. Combined with BlackRock and State Street, the "Big Three" index managers collectively control 20-30%+ of votes at major corporations including Boeing, Lockheed Martin, RTX, and Northrop Grumman.
  - Source: Multiple 2024-2025 13F and proxy analyses (PortersFiveForce, InvestGuiding) — https://portersfiveforce.com/blogs/owners/boeing ; https://investguiding.com/article/lockheed-martin-stock-ownership-who-owns-lockheed-martin-in-2024
- **Voting as a Bloc**: Academic research confirms that "for most proxy proposals, particularly those involving corporate governance, the evaluation will result in the same position being taken across all of the funds and the funds voting as a block." This means Vanguard's governance team effectively controls votes for trillions in assets with a single decision.
  - Source: Harvard Law & Economics Discussion Paper (Strine) — https://laweconcenter.law.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Strine_899.pdf

### Named Individuals
- Vanguard is a private, mutually-owned company and does not publicly disclose individual executive compensation or stock transactions to the same degree as public corporations. Specific named individual transactions for 2024-2025 were not located in public filings during this research phase.

### Contract Values & Dates
- Vanguard is not a direct defense contractor. Its influence is exercised through equity stakes and proxy voting.

### Legal Violations Suspected
- **Antitrust / Horizontal Shareholding**: Combined with BlackRock, Vanguard holds significant stakes in directly competing defense contractors (Lockheed Martin, Boeing, RTX, Northrop Grumman, General Dynamics). Economic research (Azar, Schmalz, Tecu; Elhauge) demonstrates that such common ownership reduces competitive output and raises prices — potentially violating Sherman Act Section 1 through coordinated effects.
- **Proxy Voting Coordination**: The explicit letter agreement with BlackRock to vote BlackRock shares in proportion to other shareholders raises questions about whether similar agreements exist with other portfolio companies, effectively creating a coordinated voting bloc across the economy.
- **Mirror Voting as Abdication of Fiduciary Duty**: Systematic mirror voting may constitute a breach of fiduciary duty under ERISA and the Investment Advisers Act if it results in voting contrary to the best economic interests of fund beneficiaries.
