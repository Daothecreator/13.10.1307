## COUNT I: BLACKROCK, INC. — THE $14 TRILLION GOVERNANCE OLIGOPOLY

### FINDING OF FACT 1.1

BlackRock, Inc. holds $14.04 trillion in assets under management as of Q4 2025, having crossed $12 trillion in Q2 2025, $13 trillion in Q3 2025, and $14 trillion in Q4 2025 — the first asset manager to reach these milestones. In 2025, BlackRock recorded record net inflows of $698 billion, revenue exceeding $24.2 billion (up 19% year-over-year), and operating income as adjusted of $9.6 billion (up 18%). BlackRock is a top beneficial owner of the five largest U.S. defense contractors: Lockheed Martin (7.40%, 18,292,313 shares, $4.6 billion invested), Boeing (5.2%), Northrop Grumman (5.5%), RTX/Raytheon (6.6%), and General Dynamics (3.94%). BlackRock's centralized governance team determines how all BlackRock funds vote, and the firm expanded its "Voting Choice" program to more than 3 million U.S. retail shareholder accounts invested in IVV, resulting in $3.1 trillion of eligible AUM eligible to participate in Voting Choice at end of 2024.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 1.1

These figures are established by BlackRock Q4 2025 Earnings Release (15 January 2026), SEC 10-K filings, BlackRock 2025 and 2026 Proxy Statements, Morningstar Nordics analysis, Corporate Accountability analysis of SEC 13F filings, and InvestGuiding ownership analysis. The proxy voting concentration is documented in the BlackRock 2025 Proxy Statement, Section 16(a) Disclosure.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 1.1

BlackRock's control over $14.04 trillion in assets, combined with significant cross-ownership of directly competing defense contractors, creates horizontal shareholding concerns under Clayton Act Section 7 and Sherman Act Section 1 theories. Academic literature documents that BlackRock and Vanguard routinely vote as a bloc across portfolio companies, reducing competitive incentives.

### DEMAND/ORDER 1.1

The Department of Justice Antitrust Division shall immediately initiate a Sherman Act Section 1 and Clayton Act Section 7 investigation into BlackRock's horizontal shareholding across competing defense contractors. The SEC shall audit BlackRock's proxy voting records for the period 2020–2025. Laurence D. Fink shall be indicted for willful late filing under Section 16(a) if the delayed Form 4 is found to have been within his control. BlackRock shall be required to divest all holdings in directly competing defense contractors to below 3% per firm within 180 days, or face mandatory breakup under the Sherman Act.

## COUNT III: THE VANGUARD GROUP — MIRROR VOTING AND COORDINATED GOVERNANCE

### FINDING OF FACT 3.1

The Vanguard Group, Inc. has entered into a letter agreement with BlackRock pursuant to which, for any annual or special meeting of BlackRock's shareholders, Vanguard will grant a proxy to vote any shares of BlackRock common stock owned by Vanguard in excess of 10% of the outstanding BlackRock shares in proportion to the votes of other shares. Per Amendment No. 8 to Schedule 13G filed February 13, 2024, Vanguard holds 12,258,524 shares sole dispositive power in BlackRock. Vanguard has entered into agreements with various regulators and issuers that limit the amount of shares that the funds may vote at their discretion; for these securities, any additional shares generally are "mirror voted." Academic research confirms that "for most proxy proposals, particularly those involving corporate governance, the evaluation will result in the same position being taken across all of the funds and the funds voting as a block." Combined with BlackRock and State Street, the "Big Three" index managers collectively control 20–30%+ of votes at major corporations including Boeing, Lockheed Martin, RTX, and Northrop Grumman.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 3.1

The letter agreement is disclosed in the BlackRock 2025 Proxy Statement, Schedule 13G/A disclosure. The mirror voting practices are documented in the Vanguard Market Neutral Fund Prospectus (SEC filing). The voting-as-a-bloc research is published in the Harvard Law & Economics Discussion Paper (Strine). The Big Three control percentages are established by multiple 2024–2025 13F and proxy analyses.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 3.1

The explicit letter agreement between BlackRock and Vanguard, combined with systematic mirror voting and bloc voting across trillions in assets, constitutes a de facto governance cartel. This coordination is not captured by Clayton Act Section 8 because the statute targets individual officers and directors, not passive asset managers that vote millions of shares without placing employees on boards. The result is a massive enforcement gap: the two firms can hold top positions in competing defense contractors without triggering Clayton Act liability, even as their coordinated proxy voting guidelines produce governance outcomes identical to those that would flow from interlocked directorates.

### DEMAND/ORDER 3.1

The Federal Trade Commission shall initiate a Section 5 unfair competition investigation into the BlackRock-Vanguard proxy voting coordination, treating the combined voting power as a de facto interlocking directorate. Congress shall amend Clayton Act Section 8 to explicitly include asset managers with combined voting power exceeding 15% in competing corporations as subject to interlock prohibitions.

Palantir Technologies Inc. secured a ten-year contract with the U.S. Army worth up to $10 billion in August 2025, consolidating 75 individual contract vehicles into one enterprise agreement for the Army Vantage platform. In November 2023, NHS England awarded Palantir a £330 million contract to create the Federated Data Platform. HHS renewed/expanded Tiberius to $31 million. In April 2025, WIRED reported ICE pays $30 million for "ImmigrationOS" prototype through 2027. In June 2025, Senate Finance Committee Ranking Member Ron Wyden and Rep. In June 2025, the U.S. Army swore in senior executives of the top four American AI companies, including Palantir, as lieutenant colonels in the Army Reserve. Palantir stock rose from approximately $6 (early 2023) to $187 (August 2025), with market cap reaching $440 billion.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 4.1

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 4.1

The consolidation of 75 Army contract vehicles into a single $10 billion Palantir enterprise agreement constitutes vendor lock-in and elimination of competitive bidding, in violation of the Competition in Contracting Act (41 U.S.C. § 3301 et seq.) and the Federal Acquisition Regulation. The hiring of former DoD Chief Data Officer David Spirk and the commissioning of Palantir executives as Army Reserve lieutenant colonels create potential violations of post-employment restrictions under 18 U.S.C. § 207. The rapid escalation of lobbying expenditures with majority ex-government lobbyists raises questions about compliance with the Lobbying Disclosure Act (2 U.S.C. § 1601) and the Foreign Agents Registration Act (22 U.S.C. § 611).

### DEMAND/ORDER 4.1

The U.S. Army shall immediately terminate the $10 billion Palantir enterprise agreement and re-compete all 75 contract vehicles as separate, competitively bid procurements. The DOJ shall indict David Spirk and all Palantir executives commissioned as Army Reserve officers for violations of 18 U.S.C. § 207. The SEC shall investigate Alex Karp, Stephen A. Cohen, and Peter A. Thiel for insider trading under Rule 10b5-1, and shall freeze all proceeds from their 2024–2025 stock sales pending investigation. ## COUNT VIII: THE DEFENSE CONTRACTOR CARTEL — MARKET CONCENTRATION AND PRICE GOUGING

### FINDING OF FACT 8.1

The "Big Five" U.S. defense contractors — Lockheed Martin, RTX, Northrop Grumman, Boeing, and General Dynamics — receive over 80% of defense dollars. In FY2024, Lockheed Martin received $50.75 billion in prime DoD contract obligations; RTX received $24.82 billion; Northrop Grumman received $18.58 billion; Boeing received $23.22 billion; and General Dynamics received $19.20 billion. RTX/Raytheon entered into a deferred prosecution agreement with DOJ in 2024 for bribery of a Qatar government official and conspiracy to violate the Arms Export Control Act, paying $950 million in penalties. Boeing consented to an SEC administrative order on 7 April 2025, agreeing to cease and desist from violating Section 17(a)(2) of the Securities Act of 1933 and paying a $1.5 million fine for disclosure violations. A 2023 CBS News investigation found that defense contractors routinely overcharged by 40–50%, with some markups reaching 4,451%. Northrop Grumman holds a record $95 billion backlog entrenched in Special Access Programs (SAPs), where classified contracts form an "ironclad barrier" against competitive entry or public audit. The defense sector contributed $41.4 million in the 2023–2024 election cycle; for every $1 contributed, top contractors receive approximately $2,223 in DoD contracts.

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 8.1

The contract values are established by Defense Security Monitor / USAspending.gov via ApifyForge. The RTX DPA is documented by CAAT. The Boeing SEC order is from Boeing SEC filing (Form 10-K / 8-K). The F-35 overruns are from 2021 GAO and DOD reports. The 40–50% overcharge figure is from CBS News (2023). The $95 billion Northrop backlog is from Stocks Foundry (27 January 2026) and Matrix BCG (8 March 2026). The campaign contribution and contract return ratio is from ApifyForge / OpenSecrets / USAspending.gov.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 8.1

The concentration of over 80% of U.S. defense spending in five firms, combined with the documented pattern of overcharging (40–50%, with markups up to 4,451%), constitutes a government-sponsored oligopoly in violation of the Sherman Act. The $950 million RTX deferred prosecution agreement for foreign bribery and Arms Export Control Act conspiracy establishes criminal conduct by a firm that received $24.82 billion in U.S. contracts in the same year. The $41.4 million in campaign contributions yielding approximately $2,223 per dollar in contracts represents a return on investment exceeding 222,000%, establishing prima facie evidence of quid pro quo bribery under 18 U.S.C. § 201.

### DEMAND/ORDER 8.1

The Department of Defense shall immediately break all contracts with Lockheed Martin, RTX, Northrop Grumman, Boeing, and General Dynamics and re-compete all major weapons programs under open, fixed-price competition with mandatory cost disclosure. The DOJ shall indict the CEOs and CFOs of all five firms for conspiracy to commit procurement fraud under 18 U.S.C. § 371 and for bribery of public officials under 18 U.S.C. § 201. All campaign contributions from defense contractors to members of the House and Senate Armed Services Committees shall be disgorged to the U.S. Treasury.

## COUNT IX: THE REVOLVING DOOR — SYSTEMIC CORRUPTION OF THE PROCUREMENT PROCESS

### FINDING OF FACT 9.1

The defense sector has hired more than 2,700 revolving door lobbyists since 2001. In 2023, 517 of 708 defense lobbyists had swung through the revolving door — former government officials now lobbying their former colleagues. Michelle Jelnicky, former legislative director to Rep. Jack Bergman (R-MI), left Capitol Hill in January 2023 to become Associate Director of Global Government Relations at Raytheon and immediately began lobbying Congress and DoD on the NDAA for 2024. Paul Arcangeli, former Democratic House Armed Services Committee staff director for 12 years, left in June 2022 to become principal at Invariant and started lobbying Q3 2022 for several defense clients including Raytheon; he was recognized by Rep. Adam Smith for his "indispensable role in supporting the passage of nearly one-third of every NDAA the House has ever considered."

### EVIDENTIARY BASIS 9.1

The 2,700+ revolving door figure and the 517 of 708 statistic are from Radio Rebelde / OpenSecrets (19 May 2023). Jelnicky's and Arcangeli's career trajectories are documented by OpenSecrets lobbying records and congressional press releases.

### LEGAL QUALIFICATION 9.1

The movement of 517 former government officials into defense lobbying in a single year constitutes a structural violation of 18 U.S.C. § 207 and the spirit of the Ethics in Government Act. The immediate commencement of lobbying by Jelnicky and Arcangeli upon leaving government service, targeting the same legislative vehicles they previously managed, is the wholesale capture of the legislative process by the industry it is supposed to regulate. The statement by Rep. Adam Smith that Arcangeli was indispensable to the passage of nearly one-third of all NDAAs constitutes an admission that the defense authorization process has been staffed by defense contractor employees for decades.

### DEMAND/ORDER 9.1

All 517 defense lobbyists identified as revolving door personnel in 2023 shall be permanently barred from lobbying Congress, the Department of Defense, or any federal agency on defense matters. The House and Senate Armed Services Committees shall be dissolved and reconstituted with members who have never received campaign contributions from defense contractors or employed former defense lobbyists as staff. All NDAA provisions drafted by or with the assistance of defense contractor lobbyists since 2001 shall be subject to sunset review and reauthorization under competitive bidding requirements.
