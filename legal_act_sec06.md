### COUNT I — THE PANDORA PAPERS CONSTITUTED A MASSIVE DISCLOSURE OF STATE-LEVEL WEALTH CONCEALMENT

#### FINDING OF FACT

The International Consortium of Investigative Journalists (ICIJ) published the Pandora Papers on October 3, 2021, comprising 11.9 million documents totaling 2.94 terabytes from 14 offshore service providers. The investigation mobilized more than 600 journalists across 117 countries. The dataset exposed the offshore holdings of 35 current and former world leaders, more than 330 politicians and public officials, and over 130 billionaires from 45 countries, including 46 Russian oligarchs. The combined wealth of the top 100 billionaires identified in the leak exceeded $600 billion.

King Abdullah II of Jordan was identified as the beneficiary of 16 companies established by the Panamanian law firm Alcogal, holding a $100 million property empire spanning Malibu, Washington D.C., London's Belgravia, and Ascot. A 2017 Alcogal memorandum named a client "Abdullah Al Hussein," born January 30, 1962—the King's birth date—residing at Raghadan Palace, as beneficiary of the 16 corporate vehicles. The Jordanian royal court denied misuse of public funds. ICIJ access was blocked in Jordan hours before publication.

Former UK Prime Minister Tony Blair appeared in the documents through British Virgin Islands company Romanstone International Limited, which owned a London building purchased for £6.45 million. The arrangement allowed Blair to avoid £312,000 in stamp duty. Cherie Blair stated the structure was intended to bring the company back into the UK tax regime, and Alcogal denied that either Tony or Cherie Blair were its clients.

Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš was documented using €15 million transferred between his offshore companies to acquire a chateau on the French Riviera. He failed to declare the offshore vehicle as required by Czech law.

In India, the leak named more than 300 businessmen, including associates of Anil Ambani and fugitive diamantaire Nirav Modi, revealing how offshore structures were used to manage debt-laden domestic conglomerates and evade creditor scrutiny.

The Alcogal law firm—Alemán, Cordero, Galindo & Lee—created at least 14,000 shell companies and trusts in tax havens.

#### DEMAND/ORDER

**DEMAND ONE:** The immediate revocation of all operating licenses, charters, and notarial powers of Alcogal and all 14 offshore service providers identified in the Pandora Papers by the Republic of Panama, the British Virgin Islands, Cyprus, and all other enabling jurisdictions.

**DEMAND TWO:** The arrest and extradition of all beneficial owners of the 16 Alcogal companies holding King Abdullah II's $100 million property empire for interrogation under mutual legal assistance treaties.

**DEMAND THREE:** The seizure of Romanstone International Limited and all BVI properties held by UK political figures, with immediate assessment of back-tax liability and stamp duty recovery.

**DEMAND FOUR:** The dissolution of all 14,000+ Alcogal shell companies and the public registration of their ultimate beneficial owners on open, searchable registries.

### COUNT II — CYPRUS CONFIDENTIAL DOCUMENTED REAL-TIME SANCTIONS EVASION BY SYSTEMICALLY IMPORTANT PROFESSIONAL SERVICE FIRMS

#### FINDING OF FACT

In November 2023, the ICIJ and Paper Trail Media published the Cyprus Confidential investigation, analyzing 3.6 million leaked documents from six Cyprus-based financial services providers and a Latvian firm. The investigation involved 270 journalists from 54 countries. The documents dated primarily from the mid-1990s to April 2022, covering the period before and after Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

Among the 104 Russian billionaires on the 2023 Forbes list, 67—two-thirds—appeared as clients of Cypriot professional services providers, along with their family members.

PwC Cyprus helped sanctioned oligarchs shuffle their riches as Russian tanks rolled into Ukraine. In spring 2022, scientists-turned-billionaires Alexander Abramov and Alexander Frolov rushed to move $100 million between two shell companies they controlled, with the original source traced to Evraz PLC, the steel and mining giant producing 97 percent of the metal used in Russian railways. Two months after the transaction, the British government sanctioned Evraz as "of strategic significance to the government of Russia."

PwC Cyprus and Cypcodirect—a firm founded by a former PwC partner—helped Alexey Mordashov transfer a $1.4 billion stake in German travel firm TUI Group to his wife Marina one day after EU sanctions were imposed. German authorities later blocked the transfer provisionally.

Roman Abramovich, the former Chelsea owner and sanctioned oligarch, had numerous companies and trusts managed by Cyprus firms Cypcodirect and MeritServus; the UK later sanctioned MeritServus for allegedly helping Abramovich hide assets after sanctions.

Hubert Seipel, the veteran German journalist who wrote two books about Putin with unprecedented access, received over $600,000 from a shell company linked to Mordashov, with a handwritten note on a 2018 document explaining the payment as support "for writing a book on [the] political environment in the Russian Federation."

#### EVIDENTIARY BASIS

Established by documentary evidence: 3.6 million leaked documents analyzed by 270 journalists. Verified by ICIJ, Paper Trail Media, and 54-country collaborative network. PwC Cyprus is a registered partnership with a duty of care under Cypriot and EU law. The $100 million Abramov/Frolov transfer is documented by corporate filings and bank records. The $1.4 billion Mordashov transfer is documented by share transfer agreements dated one day post-sanctions. The €600,000 Seipel payment is documented by a handwritten note on a corporate record.

#### LEGAL QUALIFICATION

The conduct documented in Cyprus Confidential constitutes:

1. **Sanctions evasion** in violation of EU Regulation 833/2014, UK Russia (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019, and U.S. Executive Orders 14024 and 13662.
2. **Professional misconduct and conspiracy** by PwC Cyprus, Cypcodirect, and MeritServus, violating the EU Directive on Administrative Cooperation (DAC6), the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law, and the ICAEW Code of Ethics.
3. **Foreign influence operations** funded through concealed channels, implicating 18 U.S.C. § 951 (foreign agent registration) and equivalent statutes in Germany and the European Union.
4. **Material support to sanctioned entities** under 50 U.S.C. § 1705 and EU blocking statutes.

PwC Cyprus is not an innocent service provider. It is a co-conspirator in wartime sanctions evasion. The "Big Four" accounting firms have operated as the compliance officers of oligarchy, certifying the financial statements of kleptocracy while facilitating its concealment.

#### DEMAND/ORDER

**DEMAND FIVE:** The immediate suspension of PwC Cyprus's audit license and the global decertification of all PwC network firms from government contracting for a period of not less than ten years.

**DEMAND SIX:** The arrest and prosecution of all PwC Cyprus partners who authorized, reviewed, or failed to report the Abramov, Frolov, Mordashov, and Abramovich transactions.

**DEMAND SEVEN:** The seizure of all Cypcodirect and MeritServus client records and their immediate transmission to the EU Sanctions Enforcement Directorate, the UK Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation, and the U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control.

**DEMAND EIGHT:** The freezing of all TUI Group shares transferred in the Mordashov transaction and their forfeiture to the German Federal Republic as proceeds of sanctions evasion.

### COUNT III — THE FINCEN FILES PROVED THAT SYSTEMICALLY IMPORTANT BANKS PROCESS TRILLIONS IN SUSPICIOUS TRANSACTIONS AS A BUSINESS MODEL

#### FINDING OF FACT

The FinCEN Files, published by BuzzFeed News and the ICIJ in September 2020, were based on more than 2,100 suspicious activity reports (SARs) leaked from the U.S. Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, covering over 200,000 suspicious financial transactions between 1999 and 2017 valued at over $2 trillion. The investigation involved more than 400 journalists from 110 news organizations across 88 countries.

Major global banks—including JPMorgan, HSBC, Standard Chartered, Deutsche Bank, and Bank of New York Mellon—continued processing transactions for powerful and dangerous players even after paying billions of dollars in fines for anti-money laundering violations.

JPMorgan transferred more than $1 billion for a key figure in Malaysia's 1MDB fraud scandal and more than $2 million for a Venezuelan energy company allegedly involved in causing electrical blackouts.

Deutsche Bank handled approximately $1.3 trillion in suspicious transactions, while JPMorgan processed $514 billion.

The academic analysis of ICIJ financial data leaks establishes that offshore service providers often performed little to no due diligence to identify the source of an investor's funds, and trust agreements permitting oral instructions for trust disbursements were used to reduce the risk of a paper trail identifying trust financial activities.

The FinCEN Files demonstrated that the SAR regime had become a defensive compliance ritual rather than an investigative trigger: banks filed reports while continuing to process transactions, creating a paper trail that satisfied regulatory requirements without interrupting the flow of illicit capital.

#### EVIDENTIARY BASIS

Established by documentary evidence: 2,100+ SARs filed by the banks themselves, constituting admissions of suspicion under 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g). Verified by BuzzFeed News, ICIJ, and 400+ journalists. The $2 trillion transaction value is aggregated from the SARs themselves. JPMorgan's $1 billion 1MDB transfer and $2 million Venezuelan transfer are documented by internal bank records. Deutsche Bank's $1.3 trillion and JPMorgan's $514 billion in suspicious transactions are self-reported figures extracted from the leaked SAR database.

#### LEGAL QUALIFICATION

The conduct documented in the FinCEN Files constitutes:

1. **Systemic money laundering** under 18 U.S.C. § 1956, 18 U.S.C. § 1957, and the Bank Secrecy Act (31 U.S.C. § 5318 et seq.).
2. **Willful blindness to criminal proceeds** under the U.S. Sentencing Guidelines § 2S1.1 and UK Proceeds of Crime Act 2002.
3. **Material support to transnational criminal organizations, corrupt foreign officials, and sanctioned regimes** under 18 U.S.C. § 2339A and Executive Order 13224.
4. **Repeated violations of deferred prosecution agreements and consent orders** by JPMorgan, HSBC, Deutsche Bank, and Standard Chartered, constituting contempt of court and breach of contractual undertakings to the U.S. Department of Justice.

The SAR regime is a sham. Banks file reports to create legal cover while continuing to profit from the transactions. This is not compliance; it is complicity dressed in paperwork. The $1.3 trillion in suspicious transactions processed by Deutsche Bank alone exceeds the annual GDP of most nations. When a bank processes $1.3 trillion in transactions it itself has flagged as suspicious, the bank is no longer a financial intermediary; it is a criminal enterprise.

#### DEMAND/ORDER

**DEMAND NINE:** The immediate revocation of the U.S. banking licenses of any institution that processed more than $100 billion in self-reported suspicious transactions without terminating the underlying client relationships.

**DEMAND TEN:** The criminal indictment of the Chief Compliance Officers and Chief Executive Officers of JPMorgan, Deutsche Bank, HSBC, Standard Chartered, and Bank of New York Mellon for willful blindness to money laundering under 18 U.S.C. § 1956.

**DEMAND ELEVEN:** The disgorgement of all fees, interest, and trading profits derived from the $2 trillion in suspicious transactions documented in the FinCEN Files, with proceeds directed to a global restitution fund for victims of corruption and kleptocracy.

**DEMAND TWELVE:** The abolition of the SAR regime in its current form and its replacement with a mandatory transaction-freeze requirement: any transaction flagged as suspicious must be frozen pending law enforcement review, not merely reported while continuing to flow.

### COUNT IV — SWISS LEAKS ESTABLISHED THAT HSBC'S SWISS PRIVATE BANK OPERATED AS A CRIMINAL FACILITY FOR TAX EVASION AND CASH SMUGGLING

#### FINDING OF FACT

The Swiss Leaks investigation, published by the ICIJ and Le Monde in February 2015, was based on a trove of almost 60,000 leaked files providing details on over 100,000 HSBC clients and their bank accounts. The data, secreted away by former HSBC employee Hervé Falciani, covered accounts holding more than $100 billion in total, with some estimates placing the maximum amounts at €180.6 billion between November 2006 and March 2007.

The files revealed that HSBC's Swiss private bank routinely allowed clients to withdraw bricks of cash, aggressively marketed tax-avoidance schemes, and provided accounts to international criminals and corrupt businessmen.

HSBC acknowledged that "the compliance culture and standards of due diligence in HSBC's Swiss private bank, as well as the industry in general, were significantly lower than they are today," and stated that its Swiss arm had reduced its client base by almost 70 percent since 2007.

In June 2015, HSBC and the Swiss prosecutor's office agreed to close the case with HSBC paying a fine of 40 million Swiss francs (€38 million).

Falciani, the whistleblower, was sentenced in absentia to five years in prison by a Swiss court in November 2015 for industrial espionage, data theft, and violating banking secrecy. Spain refused to extradite him, and he never served the sentence.

#### EVIDENTIARY BASIS

Established by documentary evidence: 60,000 leaked files verified by ICIJ and Le Monde. HSBC's own admission of deficient compliance culture is a corporate confession. The €38 million fine is a matter of public record from the Swiss prosecutor's office. Falciani's five-year sentence is a matter of public record from the Swiss Federal Criminal Court. The $100 billion in account values is documented by internal HSBC records.

#### LEGAL QUALIFICATION

The conduct documented in Swiss Leaks constitutes:

1. **Systemic tax evasion facilitation** in violation of the tax codes of France, the United Kingdom, the United States, and 180+ other jurisdictions whose citizens held undeclared accounts.
2. **Criminal conspiracy to smuggle cash** across borders, violating Article 3 of the UN Convention Against Corruption and national customs statutes.
3. **Retaliatory prosecution of a whistleblower** in violation of the Council of Europe Recommendation CM/Rec(2014)7 and the universal principle that truth-telling in the public interest is a defense to breach of confidentiality.
4. **Corporate impunity purchased through negotiated penalty** — the €38 million fine represents approximately 0.038% of the €100 billion in account values, constituting a de facto license fee for criminal conduct.

The disparity in outcomes is a verdict on the justice system itself: HSBC paid a fine equivalent to a rounding error on its quarterly profits, while the whistleblower who exposed the crimes received a five-year prison term.

#### DEMAND/ORDER

**DEMAND THIRTEEN:** The immediate arrest and extradition of all HSBC Swiss private bank executives who authorized cash withdrawals, tax-avoidance marketing, and criminal client onboarding between 2006 and 2015.

**DEMAND FOURTEEN:** The full pardon and restitution of Hervé Falciani, with the Swiss Confederation ordered to pay compensatory damages for his retaliatory prosecution and to repeal banking secrecy laws that obstruct criminal investigations.

**DEMAND FIFTEEN:** The seizure of HSBC's global assets equivalent to 10% of the $100 billion in Swiss account values — $10 billion — as a punitive penalty for systemic tax evasion facilitation, with proceeds directed to the treasuries of the nations whose citizens were victimized.

### COUNT V — THE DOUGLAS LATCHFORD CASE PROVED THAT OFFSHORE TRUSTS CONCEAL STOLEN CULTURAL PROPERTY AND CONVERT LOOTED ARTIFACTS INTO ANONYMIZED FINANCIAL ASSETS

#### FINDING OF FACT

Douglas Latchford, a British antiquities dealer specializing in Khmer artifacts, was indicted by U.S. authorities in 2019 for conspiracy and trafficking in stolen and looted Cambodian antiquities. He died in 2020 before standing trial.

The leaked documents revealed that Latchford and his family set up secret offshore trusts in tax havens shortly after he was linked to looted antiquities, using trusts and offshore accounts to store antiquities and hold profits from their sale.

Two trusts—Skanda Trust and Siva Trust—were used to obscure ownership of artifacts, with records showing antiquities marked in Latchford's books as "courtesy of Skanda Trust" that could be linked to looting.

The trustee for both trusts was another private trust company the Latchfords had registered to give themselves an added layer of privacy.

In 2021, Latchford's daughter, Julia Latchford Copleston, agreed to return 125 Khmer objects valued at approximately $50 million from her father's private collection to Cambodia, along with Champa sculptures returned to Vietnam.

The Denver Art Museum agreed to return four artifacts to Cambodia after the Pandora Papers revealed their link to Latchford. The Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York holds at least 12 pieces once owned or brokered by Latchford, and the British Museum, Cleveland Museum of Art, Museum of Fine Arts Boston, and Asian Art Museum San Francisco also hold artifacts directly linked to him.

#### EVIDENTIARY BASIS

Established by documentary evidence: U.S. Department of Justice indictment of Douglas Latchford, 2019. Verified by ICIJ Pandora Papers analysis, Antiquities Coalition investigations, and museum restitution records. The Skanda Trust and Siva Trust are registered entities with documentary presence in the leaked files. The $50 million valuation is established by Cambodian government and museum appraisal records. The 12 pieces at the Metropolitan Museum are catalogued in public collection databases.

#### LEGAL QUALIFICATION

The conduct documented in the Latchford case constitutes:

1. **Conspiracy to traffic in stolen cultural property** in violation of the U.S. National Stolen Property Act (18 U.S.C. § 2314), the UNESCO 1970 Convention, and national patrimony laws of Cambodia and Vietnam.
2. **Money laundering of proceeds of cultural theft** under 18 U.S.C. § 1956 and the UK Proceeds of Crime Act 2002.
3. **Fraudulent concealment of criminal assets through offshore trusts** in violation of trust law fiduciary duties and anti-money laundering statutes.
4. **Receipt of stolen property by major museums** — the Metropolitan Museum of Art, British Museum, Cleveland Museum of Art, Museum of Fine Arts Boston, Denver Art Museum, and Asian Art Museum San Francisco — constituting knowing possession of looted artifacts and conversion of state property.

**DEMAND SIXTEEN:** The immediate seizure and repatriation of all Latchford-linked artifacts held by the Metropolitan Museum of Art, British Museum, Cleveland Museum of Art, Museum of Fine Arts Boston, and Asian Art Museum San Francisco, with criminal investigation of curators and acquisition committees who approved their purchase.

**DEMAND SEVENTEEN:** The dissolution of Skanda Trust, Siva Trust, and all affiliated private trust companies, with public disclosure of all settlors, beneficiaries, and asset schedules.

**DEMAND EIGHTEEN:** The extension of the Latchford indictment posthumously to his estate, trustees, and family members who administered the concealment architecture, with civil forfeiture of all remaining trust assets.

### COUNT VI — THE ENFORCEMENT GAP IS STRUCTURAL: INSTITUTIONAL ENABLERS FACE NO CONSEQUENCES

#### FINDING OF FACT

By 2025, the Pandora Papers had triggered a wave of investigations and limited accountability actions, but the systemic offshore infrastructure remained intact. In Ecuador, President Guillermo Lasso faced investigation for tax fraud. In Mexico, the country's anti-money laundering chief resigned in November 2021 amid scandal. In Chile, President Sebastián Piñera defeated impeachment charges sparked by the leak, with the Senate acquitting him in November 2021. In Brazil, the Pandora Papers implicated President of the Central Bank Roberto Campos Neto and Minister of Economy Paulo Guedes, who purportedly held $10 million in offshore accounts in the British Virgin Islands; critics called for their resignation, though neither was removed.

A bipartisan group of U.S. lawmakers introduced the ENABLERS Act, which would expand the Bank Secrecy Act reporting requirements to lawyers, accountants, and trust companies. As of 2026, the legislation had not been enacted.

On June 25, 2025, FinCEN issued three orders designating CIBanco S.A., Intercam Banco S.A., and Vector Casa de Bolsa S.A. de C.V. as "primary money laundering concerns" under the Fentanyl Sanctions Act and FEND Off Fentanyl Act, effective September 4, 2025.

The cumulative record reveals consistent asymmetry: individual actors face political consequences, while institutional enablers face none. Alcogal continues to operate. PwC Cyprus remains a Big Four firm. HSBC remains one of the world's largest banks. Mossack Fonseca dissolved only after its brand became toxic; its founders were acquitted in June 2024, and its partners reconstituted under new names.

#### EVIDENTIARY BASIS

Established by documentary evidence: Congressional records of ENABLERS Act introduction. Verified by FinCEN public orders, June 25, 2025. The continued operation of Alcogal, PwC Cyprus, and HSBC is verified by corporate registry searches and regulatory filings as of 2026. The acquittal of Mossack Fonseca founders is a matter of public record from Panamanian courts, June 2024.

#### LEGAL QUALIFICATION

The enforcement gap is not a resource constraint. It is a structural feature of regulatory capture. The institutions that enable offshore secrecy are too embedded in the global financial system to be dismantled without systemic disruption, and the political will to do so is absent because the same elites who benefit from secrecy are the ones who would have to authorize its destruction.

This constitutes:

1. **Regulatory capture** of national and international enforcement bodies by the financial services industry.
2. **Obstruction of justice** through the deliberate non-enforcement of existing statutes.
3. **Legislative sabotage** — the ENABLERS Act has been blocked by lobbying pressure from the American Bar Association, American Institute of CPAs, and other professional guilds whose members profit from opacity.

#### DEMAND/ORDER

**DEMAND NINETEEN:** The immediate enactment of the ENABLERS Act or equivalent legislation in all G7 jurisdictions, with criminal penalties for lawyers, accountants, and trust administrators who fail to file SARs equivalent to those imposed on banks.

**DEMAND TWENTY:** The establishment of an International Offshore Dismantlement Tribunal with authority to revoke corporate charters, freeze assets, and extradite enablers across all jurisdictions, operating without regard to sovereign immunity claims from financial secrecy jurisdictions.

**DEMAND TWENTY-ONE:** The public naming and professional decertification of all lawyers, accountants, and trust officers who created, administered, or failed to report the 14,000+ Alcogal shells, the PwC Cyprus sanctions-evasion structures, and the HSBC Swiss criminal accounts.

**DEMAND TWENTY-TWO:** The termination of all government contracts with HSBC, PwC, and Deloitte in all jurisdictions where they have been documented facilitating tax evasion, sanctions evasion, or money laundering, for a period of not less than 15 years.

Their corporate registries, trust laws, and banking secrecy statutes are void as against the universal interest in suppressing transnational financial crime.
